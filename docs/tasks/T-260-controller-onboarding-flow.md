# T-260 — Controller onboarding: USB detection, first-run guidance, one-click pairing, the paired list

Status: in-progress · Phase: 2 · Depends: T-203, T-199 · Area: shell, services · Size: M · Owner: builder

## Goal
The first minutes with a controller just work and explain themselves (owner request, 2026-11-07, "Controller onboarding experience"): plug a pad in and the console says what it is; a console with no pad tells you how to pair yours, per family; the paired pads are listed and reconnect by themselves. This task covers only the **gaps**; the table says what exists.

## What exists or is planned (do not duplicate)

## Scope
1. **(a) A controller appeared:** a toast "<Name> connected" (USB or Bluetooth) with the family glyph (T-199) and the battery when known, once per connection (a debounce for a flaky cable), and "<Name> disconnected" only when the pad was in use (quiet otherwise). The platform event comes from `ruya-inputd`/`ruya-devices` (extend the contract first if the shell cannot tell a pad from a keyboard or mouse); the shell shows nothing for keyboards and mice here.
2. **(b) First-run guidance:** where T-203 shows its "no controller" state (wizard step, picker, empty Settings), show **per-family steps** in plain words, with our own simple drawings (no vendor art or marks): PlayStation-style ("hold PS and Create until the light bar flashes"), Xbox-style ("press the pairing button on top until the logo flashes fast"), 8BitDo ("hold Start and the Y/X combination for your mode; use the Bluetooth or X-input mode": text from `T-261`'s findings), Switch-style, "other". While the screen is open the pairing window of T-203 is open and discovery runs; each discovered input device appears in a list as it is found, with its family glyph, and **one A press pairs it** (progress, success with "Connected, remembered", failure with a reason and Retry). Controller-only, B backs out.
3. **(c) "Press a button to try":** after a pad connects during onboarding, a small screen shows the pad's buttons and sticks lighting up as pressed (a light version of T-217's button test: reuse its component if it exists, else build the minimal one and leave T-217 to extend it) and "Continue".
4. **(d) The paired list** in Settings -> Bluetooth (controllers section, or a Controllers pane if T-217 made one): each paired pad with name, family glyph, battery, state (Connected / Reconnects automatically / Not in range), **Rename** (on-screen keyboard, stored by BlueZ alias) and **Forget** (confirmation naming the pad); a "Pair a new controller" row that opens (b).
5. Mock and tests: scripted USB connect, pairing success, timeout, rejected device class; Turkish; accessible names.

## Outcome
Verified: host (hardware pending: the Dell checks below). Rust tests, QML tests (offscreen) and screenshots ran on the host; no adapter, no real BlueZ and no USB pad were involved.

**What was built**

- **Contract (`api/dbus/org.ruyaos.Devices1.xml`, Bluetooth part only):** `Rename(id, name) -> status` (BlueZ `Alias`); signals `GamepadConnected` / `GamepadDisconnected(id, name, family, transport)`; the `family` value `8bitdo`. Statuses reuse the existing ones (`invalid` also for a bad name).
- **Service (`services/ruya-devices`):** `input.rs` now reads `uniq`, `phys` and the `id/*` files of every input device and follows the game controllers (`gamepad_of`, `Gamepad`, `GamepadEvent`, `InputPresence::take_gamepad_events`): the first read is the baseline, one pad with several input nodes is one pad, the id is the Bluetooth address else the USB port else the name, so a re-plugged cable keeps it. `dbus.rs` emits the signals from the existing two-second `watch_input` loop and has `Rename`; `bluetooth.rs` has `rename` (trim, 48 characters, no control characters) and the `Bluez::set_alias` seam (fake, `BluezClient`, `DynBluez`); `families.rs` has `8bitdo` (USB vendor `0x2DC8`, or the name).
- **Shell:** `Bluetooth` facade and backends (`rename`, the two signals, mock `mockReveal`, `mockGamepad`). New `ControllerToasts.qml` (hosted in `ToastLayer`, fed key activity by `Shell` through `ToastLayer.noteInput()`), `ControllerOnboarding.qml` (the guided screen), `ButtonTest.qml` (try-a-button), per-family steps in `ControllerPairHint` (`familySteps`, `stepsFor`, `titleFor`, `showFamilies`, `onPurpose`; shown in the wizard stage and in the picker), the `DeviceIcons` word for 8BitDo, `BluetoothPane` (the "Pair a new controller" row opens the screen, "Reconnects automatically", "Rename <name>" rows for controllers, "Forget <name>" now asks first). Turkish and English strings are translated.

**How the scope items were met**

- (a) Toasts: "<Name> connected" with the family glyph and the battery when known (the Bluetooth device's level by address, else the only controller UPower lists); a 0.8 s debounce cancels a flapping cable in both directions; "<Name> disconnected" only after some key reached the shell while connected, or when no input device is left. Keyboards and mice cannot reach it: the service reports pads only (Rust `only_a_pad_is_a_pad`), and a shell test shows a connected keyboard and mouse in the list produce no toast.
- (b) Per-family steps (PlayStation, Xbox, Nintendo, 8BitDo, other) in the wizard stage and the picker (all five listed, since nobody can pick) and in the guided screen (a family row, Left/Right; the steps of the family in focus). While the screen is open the T-203 window is open; its security rules are untouched (no change to the window, the agent, the class filter, the cap or the sweep). Found controllers appear in place with their family glyph; A pairs (progress, "Connected, remembered", or the reason and Retry); the list keeps its focus; B closes.
- (c) `ButtonTest`: lights the D-pad/left stick (arrows), A, LB and RB while held and marks them once tried; Start continues, B closes. **Limit:** the shell sees a pad only as the keys `ruya-inputd` makes of it, so X, Y, the triggers and the right stick cannot be shown; that needs raw buttons (inbox line for T-217). No T-217 component existed, so this is the minimal one, built to be extended.
- (d) The paired list: glyph, name, battery, state, Rename (on-screen keyboard, BlueZ alias), Forget (confirmation naming the pad, Cancel first), "Pair a new controller". **Not shown:** "Not in range": BlueZ gives no range information, so a trusted pad that is off says "Reconnects automatically" and an untrusted one "Paired".

**Deviations and judgement calls**

- The T-203 "Pair a controller" toggle row is replaced by "Pair a new controller" (the screen opens and closes the window); the hint row under it is gone (the screen has the words).
- "Disconnected only when in use" is approximated by "any key reached the shell while the pad was connected" (the shell cannot attribute a key to a device); a keyboard press also counts.
- The family `8bitdo` is new in `families.rs` (T-261 owns that table; the change is one row and one name). An 8BitDo pad in X-input or Switch mode reports Microsoft's or Nintendo's vendor id and shows as that family; only D-input mode reports `0x2DC8`. The 8BitDo wording (Start with X or Y, then the pair button) is from the pads' manuals and was not tried: T-261 should confirm it on the owner's pad.
- No new icon: 8BitDo and "other" use the generic gamepad glyph.
- `ControllerPairHint.message` keeps the generic words and the DualSense combination; the family lines are added under them.
- A Bluetooth keyboard connecting is not announced (the contract's signal is for pads).

**Hardware checks for the lead (the Dell, new image; nothing here ran on a real adapter or a real USB pad)**

1. *USB toast.* With the shell running and a DualSense on its cable: plug it in. Within about 3 s a toast "PlayStation controller connected" with the PS glyph and, if UPower lists the pad, " · battery N%". Watch `busctl --system monitor org.ruyaos.Devices1` for `GamepadConnected` (id `usb-...`, family `playstation`, transport `usb`). Press a few keys with the pad, unplug it: "... disconnected". Plug in and unplug without touching anything: no "disconnected". Wiggle the cable: no toast, or exactly one. `journalctl -u ruya-devices -b | grep "input changes"` must be empty.
2. *Guided pairing from a removed bond.* `bluetoothctl remove <MAC>`; Settings -> Bluetooth & controllers -> "Pair a new controller": the PlayStation steps show and the window is open (`ControllerPairingState` is `open`). Hold PS + Create: the pad appears under "Found nearby" with its glyph within about 20 s (or the window pairs it on its own and the row says "Connected, remembered"); A pairs; `bluetoothctl info` says Paired, Bonded and Trusted yes; A again opens the try screen, pressing D-pad, A, LB and RB lights them, Start closes. Repeat with an Xbox or 8BitDo pad if one is at hand and compare the wording.
3. *Rename and Forget.* On the paired list: Rename opens the keyboard with the current name; `bluetoothctl info` shows the new `Alias`; the row and the toasts use it. Forget asks "Forget <name>?" with Cancel focused; confirming removes the pad (`bluetoothctl devices Paired`).
4. *Reconnect.* Reboot; before the pad is switched on its row says "Reconnects automatically"; switch it on: the row says Connected within seconds and a toast names the pad.
5. Most likely to need a fix: `uniq` and the `id/*` files of a Bluetooth pad (a DualSense over Bluetooth should be bus `0005` with `uniq` = its address; with an empty `uniq` the id is `phys` and the alias lookup falls back to the family's words), the same pad appearing twice (USB and Bluetooth at once are two ids), and the battery being UPower's only controller rather than this one when two pads are connected.
