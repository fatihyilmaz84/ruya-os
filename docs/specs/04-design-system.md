# RuyaOS — Design system ("Dreamglass")

Status: **v1.1, Accepted (owner, 2026-10-28; ADR-012)** · Parent: [00-overview](00-overview.md) · Applies to: [02-shell](02-shell.md)

The shell's visual language: what it looks like, which tokens exist, which components exist, and how the system may change. `02-shell.md` says how the shell **behaves**; this file says how it **looks**. Like every spec it describes what is built and what is planned, and says which is which (**built** = in `Theme.qml` / `shell/qml` today; **planned (T-nnn)** = agreed, a task builds it; **proposed** = drawn, no task yet, don't build).

Visual reference: the owner's design canvas "RuyaOS Design Language" (claude.ai artifact, private to the owner; boards: Foundations, Type/space/shape, Focus/depth/motion, Home (art-led), Quick menu over a full-screen game, Launch/play/return flow, Settings, system components, art and play components). **This file wins** when the two disagree; whoever changes one updates the other in the same session (§8).

## 1. Principles

Rüya is Turkish for *dream*: a dark room lit by the thing you are about to play. The game's art fills the screen, the chrome floats over it on Gaussian-blurred glass, and one bright white ring says where you are. When a game runs, it owns the whole screen.

1. **Read from the sofa.** 3 m from a 1080p TV: body text 30 px, nothing under 24 px (badges excepted, §3.2). Text scale (100–200 %) multiplies every type step.
2. **Focus is never ambiguous.** The focused item always has the white ring (and the 1.08 scale for cards), at every quality tier and under reduced motion.
3. **Art first.** Large key art is the main visual (Home hero, art cards, launch splash). Chrome is white text on blurred glass over it; colour comes from art, avatars and the category tints, never from buttons or text.
4. **Apps own the screen.** Apps and games run full screen with no shell chrome; the Guide button is the one way back (§5.1). Motion is ease-out, under 300 ms for anything the user waits on (launch excepted), and off under reduced motion.
5. **Degrade, never break.** Blur and shadows are the default look wherever the GPU supports them (owner, 2026-11-06): richer depth is a goal, not something to trade away early. The tiers are the fallback for hardware that can't carry them: the Performance tier swaps blur for opaque glass and drops shadows; layout, sizes and focus are identical on every tier.

## 2. Units and layout

- **Design pixels:** every size is written at 1080p and multiplied by `u` (`height / 1080`), so the same numbers serve 720p and 4K (`shell/CLAUDE.md`).
- **UI size (built, T-198).** `u` is `height / 1080 * Theme.uiScale` (`Shell.u`). `Theme.uiScale` is `Display.uiScaleFactor`: the owner's choice in Settings -> Display & motion -> **UI size** (Auto, 100, 125, 150, 200 %; 100 % is the 1080p reference whatever the resolution), or on **Auto** what the screen's physical size asks for (`Display.autoScaleFor`, from the EDID size the service reports; 1 when the screen does not say). The reference is the design target: a 55" TV (685 mm high) read from 3 m is 1. **A TV (diagonal of 42" or more) is always 1**: the bigger it is, the farther away it is watched, so the UI stays a fixed fraction of the screen. A monitor of 27" or less is read from 0.8 m, and between 27" and 42" the distance grows in a straight line up to 3 m; its factor is `(685 mm / screen height) x (distance / 3 m)`, in steps of 5 %, at most 1 and at least 0.5 (a 32" 4K monitor: 0.85, a 27": 0.55). Nothing is enlarged on Auto, because the layouts are made for 1080 design pixels of height and do not make room for fewer; the owner's 125, 150 and 200 % do enlarge, and the pages then scroll or crop what no longer fits (at 150 % on 1080p the Settings pane shows three rows, at 200 % it has room for one: pages that adapt below 1080 design pixels of height are not built, and a size that is too big is undone with **Reset display settings**, a row that is always there, or by cycling the focused UI size row back to Auto). This is separate from the accessibility **text scale** (`§1`, built later), which multiplies type only. **Why the Theme unit and not `QT_SCALE_FACTOR`:** it applies live (no restart, so no black-screen risk and no second "keep" dialog), every size already goes through `u`, and the safe area, the focus rings and the hairlines follow it; `QT_SCALE_FACTOR` is read at start, would need the same restart-and-confirm as a mode change, and would also change the logical window size the layouts divide by.
- **Safe area:** the outer margin is `Theme.safeArea` (overscan, default 5 % = 96 × 54 px at 1080p), never a spacing token. Nothing focusable or readable sits outside it; backdrops and scrims fill the whole screen.
- **Home (art-led, built T-146):** full-bleed hero art of the focused game (L0) with a left legibility scrim (`heroScrim`) and a bottom fade; top bar in the safe area: section nav (Home, Games, Media, Store, Settings icon) left, status cluster (device batteries, profile, clock) right, both glass pills; the same top bar stays on the four section pages (T-168, lead decision 2026-11-03) and is hidden on detail pages, modals, the picker and the wizard; hero block left (eyebrow, title `typeHero` Bold, meta, Play + Details); the Recent row of art cards along the bottom; hint bar under it (built, T-158; the Recent row sits above it, both inside the safe area). As built: the status cluster is one chip per wireless device that reports a battery (T-223, `BatteryChip.qml`: the icon of the device's kind, a battery glyph that matches the level (§3.7) and the percentage, lowest battery first; a device discharging at 15 % or less in the warning colour with the alert glyph; at most three chips (as many as fit between the nav and the right margin; the profile name elides to about six characters first), the rest as one "+N"; a device that is 100 % on the charger has no chip; no names, only icons, so the cluster stays narrow; hidden without such a device; never focusable), the profile and the clock, and the Settings pill is the `settings` icon from the bundled font (T-157), outlined, filled while Settings is the current section.
- **Pages** (Settings, Store, …): top bar as on Home, page title `typeTitle`, content, hint bar. Content ends above the hint bar: `Page.qml` keeps a bottom margin of the safe area plus `glyphSize + spaceM`, reserved even when `hints` is empty. The backdrop stays the last hero art, blurred and dimmed. Two-pane pages (Settings): the category list shows whole rows only: when the nine categories (72 px + `spaceXs`) don't fit above the hint bar it ends at the last row that does and scrolls by rows with the selection (T-175); list 520 px, gap `spaceXxl`, pane on a `radiusL` glass panel with `spaceXl` padding.
- **Running app:** nothing of the shell is drawn except the quick menu (Guide), the volume OSD and critical toasts (§5.1).

## 3. Tokens (`shell/qml/Theme.qml`)

Components use tokens only; `scripts/lint-tokens.ps1` fails on literal colours (hex, colour names, `Qt.rgba`, `Qt.hsla`; `transparent` is allowed) and durations (font sizes too, since T-142; spacing and radii: T-143) anywhere but `Theme.qml` and the theme sets in `qml/themes/` (T-209). Names below are the `Theme` property names. **The values in the tables are Dreamglass's**, the default colour theme (§3.1, Themes); the other themes change the colours only.

**Themes (built, T-209).** The colours above are the *Dreamglass* set. `shell/qml/themes/` holds the data: `ThemeSet.qml` (every themeable token as a `required property`, so a theme that forgets one does not compile), `Themes.qml` (the five sets and `tokenNames`; `lint-tokens` checks that the list and `ThemeSet.qml` agree) and `ThemeMath.qml` (colour blending, the backdrop formula and the WCAG arithmetic, shared by the shell and the gate). `Theme.qml` keeps its property names and drives them from the current set, so no screen changed. The stored choice is `DeviceSettings.theme` (per device, `display/theme`, default `dreamglass`; an unknown stored value falls back to the default).

| Theme | Look |
|---|---|
| **Dreamglass** (default) | Night-blue glass, the look the shell shipped with |
| **Midnight** | True black ground for OLED panels, neutral grey glass, blobs at 45 % |
| **Aurora** | Deep teal ground, teal-tinted glass, violet accents |
| **Ember** | Warm near-black, copper glass, red and orange accents |
| **Daylight** | Light: dark text (`#0f1420`) on frosted white glass over a pale backdrop; white washes instead of night scrims; a dark focus ring; darker status colours; a light danger fill; shadows at 45 % |

*Themeable* (in every set): the ground and panel colours, text and textDim, the glass fills, borders, sheen, lit rim, `glassTint`, `glassSpecular`, the focus and selection fills, scrims, the hero and caption washes, `primaryFill`/`onPrimary`, `dangerFill`, `errorText`/`warningText`, the toast and status colours, `defaultTint` and the five tile accents, `blobHighlight`, `shadowColor`, and four numbers: `blobStrength` (the three Backdrop blobs' opacities), `shadowStrength` (the alphas of the shadow tokens), `lightness` (0 dark, 1 light: switches `Backdrop`'s gradient from "tint darkened" to "tint washed into the pale ground") and `artLightTop`/`artLightBottom` (the art placeholder gradient, so dark text still reads on it in Daylight). *Not themeable* (identical in all, asserted by `tst_themes.qml`): sizes, spacing, radii, type, weights, motion and easing, the focus ring **width and offset** (the ring *colour* is white on the dark themes and dark on Daylight, where white would vanish; it is never the only signal, §4), the white art effects (`cardSheen`, `glarePeak`, `shimmerPeak`: they sit on art, not on glass), `disabledOpacity`, `peekOpacity`, the grain. `glassSheenEnd` (white at alpha 0) is also fixed in all themes; the clear ends of the hero and caption fades are `heroScrimClear` and `artCaptionFadeClear` (the set's wash colour at alpha 0, so a gradient never fades through black).

*Switching.* Settings -> Display & motion -> **Theme** (the last row) cycles the five themes with Left/Right and applies at once. Every colour token cross-fades over `tintMs` (700 ms; none under reduced motion, where the switch is immediate); a switch in mid-fade starts from the blended look, so nothing jumps. Focus does not move. The first-boot wizard and the profile picker use the stored device theme. Per-profile themes are a later step (Inbox).

Contrast (WCAG, measured) of Dreamglass: `text` on `background` 18.3:1, on `panel` 15.3:1; `textDim` 8.7:1 on `background`, 7.3:1 on `panel`; `errorText` 8.8:1. **`textDim` only on opaque surfaces** (`background`, `panel`, `glassOpaque`, scrims): on glass over a bright tint it falls to about 2.8:1, so text on the shelf or over the backdrop is `text`. Status colours are never the only signal: a toast has a title word, a row has a message.

### 3.2 Type (built, T-142)

Typeface: **Inter** (OFL-1.1), bundled as a Qt resource (owner, 2026-10-28; T-145): static Regular, Light, SemiBold and Bold of Inter 4.1, subset to Latin + Latin Extended-A (`shell/fonts/Inter-*.ttf`, licence `OFL-Inter.txt`, cut by `scripts/fetch-inter.sh` from the pinned release zip, SHA-256 checked). `main.cpp` and the test host register them and make Inter the application font, so every Text inherits it; `scripts/check-font-coverage.py` (part of `dev.ps1 test`, and run by the cut script) fails when a character in `shell/qml`, `shell/src` or `shell/i18n` is missing from a cut, so a new string cannot fall back to another typeface silently; add the range to `scripts/fetch-inter.sh` and re-cut. Game titles in the hero use `typeHero` at Bold with -2 % tracking. Seven steps replace the twelve sizes that were in use (20, 26, 28, 30, 32, 34, 36, 38, 44, 56, 64, 96). Sizes outside the table were mapped to the nearest step in T-142: 40 (the keyboard entry text) and 36 (key caps) to `typeHeadline`, 52 to `typeTitle`, 84 (wizard glyphs) to `typeDisplay`. Tokens: `typeHero`..`typeMicro`, `weightLight`/`weightRegular`/`weightDemiBold`/`weightBold`, `fontFamily` (`"Inter"`). Call sites write `Theme.typeBody * u` and set `font.weight` themselves (Light for Hero and Display, DemiBold for Title, Headline and Micro; T-144); `scripts/lint-tokens.ps1` fails on a `Theme.typeHero/Display/Title/Headline/Micro` size in a block with no `font.weight` of its own: a `font.weight` in a nested child item or in a comment does not count, and a `font.pixelSize:` whose `Theme.type*` is on the next line is checked too (the self-check in `dev.ps1 test` has a fixture for each). De-emphasis is by weight or size, never a dimmer colour on glass. `google-noto-sans-fonts` stays in the image (lead, 2026-11-05) as the fallback for Flatpak apps and scripts that Inter's Latin cut does not cover; the shell itself never relies on it.

### 3.3 Spacing, shape, size (built, T-143)

- **Spacing** (8 px grid): `spaceXxs` 4, `spaceXs` 8, `spaceS` 16, `spaceM` 24, `spaceL` 36, `spaceXl` 48, `spaceXxl` 64, `spaceXxxl` 96. Rows in a list `spaceS`; tiles in a row `spaceL`; panel padding `spaceXl`; between sections `spaceXxl`.
- **Radius:** `radiusS` 12 (keys), `radiusM` 18 (tiles, art, toasts, the text-entry field), `radiusL` 32 (panels, dialogs); pills are `height / 2` (rows, buttons, badges, OSD).
- **Sizes:** `rowHeight` 84, `buttonHeight` 76, `tileWidth` 400 × `tileHeight` 260, `avatarSize` 200, `glyphSize` 40, `focusRingWidth` 4, `focusRingOffset` 7, `hairline` 1 (use `Theme.hairlineAt(u)`, never thinner than a pixel: unfocused glass, buttons and neutral badges).

### 3.4 Motion (built)

`focusMs` 140, `glideMs` 280 (the gliding highlight, T-205), `pageMs` 300 (slide `pageSlide` 60), `overlayMs` 200, `tintMs` 700 (also the hero art cross-fade), `launchMs` 400 (planned, T-147), pulse 0.94 over 70 + 180 ms, wrong-PIN shake 18 px over 45 / 90 ms, glare `glareMs` 550, ambient drift 18–29 s periods. **Every duration token is its base value times `motionScale`** (T-204): `motionScale` is `motionFactor` (a writable `Theme` property, default 1.0, the speed knob) and 0 under reduced motion, so reduced motion still zeroes every duration. Easing (built, T-204): `easeGlide` (cubic-bezier 0.25, 1, 0.5, 1) for the card focus lift (`ArtCard`, the Store card, `ProfileCard`), `easeSnap` (0.23, 1, 0.32, 1) for the page slide and fade and for the overlay and modal fades (Shell pages, the Guide overlay, PIN pad, keyboard, power and heat dialogs, app detail); both are `Easing.Bezier` arrays, used as `easing.type: Easing.Bezier; easing.bezierCurve: Theme.easeSnap`. `OutCubic` stays for toasts, the volume OSD, toggles and list scrolling until each screen is revisited; `InOutQuad` for tints and the glare, `InOutSine` for drift, `OutQuad`/`OutBack` for the pulse (`OutBack` with `easing.overshoot: popOvershoot` 0.94, about 3 %). Reduced motion: every duration 0, no pulse, no shake, no drift, no glare, no shimmer (`02-shell.md` §3.2). Only `opacity`, `scale`, `x`/`y` and colour animate.

### 3.5 Depth

Shadows belong to depth (focused and raised elements, glass panels, modals) and are on at the High and Balanced tiers, off at Performance (`Theme.shadowsOn`). Built in T-204 as the tokens `shadowLift`, `shadowPanel`, `shadowPill` (numbers in §3.8) and drawn by `GlassShadow.qml`: a black rounded rectangle in a hidden layer under the surface and one `MultiEffect` shadow over it (`blurMax` 64), (the focused card, the glass pill, the Guide panel, a toast). A card's shadow exists only while the card is focused, so at most one lift shadow is created at a time, and the lift shadow follows the art's scale (the Accept pulse). The source shape is cut out again by an inverted mask of the same rounded rectangle, so only the shadow outside the surface is drawn: it can never darken a translucent surface or show as a frame while the owner scales or fades. The fade-out is real: the layers stay alive until opacity reaches 0. Reduced motion does not remove shadows, it only makes their fade instant. Where: lift on `ArtCard` and the Store card; `shadowPanel` under the Guide panel and toasts; `shadowPill` under `GlassPill` (section nav, status cluster). Effects are never removed on hardware that supports them just to keep the implementation simple.

| Layer | z | Contents | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| L0 | backdrop / app | Hero art (Home), the **wallpaper** when the focused item has no art (Home) and blurred on pages, else the tinted blobs (the default look), or **the running app** | One shared blur, never stacked |
| L1 | 0 | Home shelf, pages, panes (glass) | |
| L2 | 10 | Toasts | Never take focus |
| L3 | 20 | Modals: picker, setup, PIN pad, keyboard, Guide overlay (scrim + `panel`) | Accept every key |
| L4 | 30 | Volume OSD | Never takes focus |
| L5 | 40 | Mode confirm ("Keep this resolution?", T-198), above the Volume OSD and the hint bar | Accepts every key; the hint bar shows its own hints |

**Wallpaper (built, T-208).** The owner can choose a picture for L0 in Settings -> Display & motion (`DeviceSettings.wallpaper`: empty = the default look, the animated blobs; a bundled id; or `user:<file>`). `WallpaperLayer.qml` sits in L0 between `Backdrop` and the hero art: two `Image`s decode asynchronously at the screen size (`sourceSize`, `PreserveAspectCrop`, `cache: false`); a new picture loads into the idle one and fades in over `tintMs` while the old one stays underneath, and is released (its source cleared) the moment the new one covers, so one decoded wallpaper is in memory and two only while changing. The blobs stop (`Backdrop.shown`) while a picture covers them. **Home:** the picture shows wherever the focused item has no art (the hero `ArtLayer` is `bare`: its fallback colour field is clear), under the left hero scrim and the bottom fade, which are unchanged; an item with art keeps its art-led hero. **Pages:** one `MultiEffect` blurs a quarter-size `ShaderEffectSource` of the pictures (`blurMax` per tier, 64 High, 32 Balanced), cross-fading in over `pageMs` when a page opens: this is the one shared blurred copy of L0, and the glass pills sample it. **Performance tier:** no blur item exists; the picture stays sharp and its dim is at least `Theme.wallpaperDimUnblurred` (35 %) on pages. **Dim and legibility (§7):** a layer of the theme's `background` colour lies over the picture (so it is a dark veil under the dark themes and a light wash under Daylight, and it cross-fades with the theme, `Theme.mix`). Its alpha is the larger of the owner's "Dim wallpaper" (0 to 60 %, default 20 %, `DeviceSettings.wallpaperDim`) and the rule. Each picture has a `peak` and a `floor`, the 95th and 5th percentile of the relative luminance of a 64x36 reduction (in `assets/wallpapers/catalog.json` for the bundled ones, in the library's `index.json` for copies). `Wallpapers.requiredDim(peak, floor, background, text)` is the least alpha at which the theme's `text` keeps 4.5:1 (computed against 4.52 so colour rounding stays above it) over the picture's worst part, modelled as a grey of that luminance composited with the layer in sRGB, as the scene graph blends it: the `peak` when the text is lighter than the background (the four dark themes), the `floor` when it is darker (Daylight); at most 85 %. Dark themes (Dreamglass numbers): Dusk, Aurora, Ember, Lagoon, Orchid need nothing beyond the default 20 % and show 12.7, 11.9, 10.0, 8.7, 11.1 : 1; Daybreak (peak 0.82) is 1.09:1 raw and gets 54 % for 4.52:1 (the layer is `#05070d`, not black: a black model said 52 % and 4.52:1, the real layer at 52 % is 4.30:1); pure white needs 58 %. Daylight turns it round: the five dark pictures get a light wash of 44 to 49 % (dark text over their darkest part 4.52:1), Daybreak none (12.6:1 at 20 %). `tst_wallpaper.qml` holds every bundled picture to 4.5:1 under every theme. So the slider is a minimum: a bright picture under a dark theme, or a dark one under Daylight, is always veiled hard. The layer's alpha is a worst case over the whole picture, not where the text sits. Transparent pixels of a copied PNG are flattened onto the theme's background at the time of the copy. A picture that cannot be loaded, or an id that is gone, makes the layer emit `failed`; the shell logs it, shows the toast "Wallpaper could not be loaded" and resets the setting to the default look. **Sources:** six bundled 1920x1080 JPEGs (`dusk`, `aurora`, `ember`, `lagoon`, `orchid`, `daybreak`, 30-36 KiB each) generated by `scripts/make-wallpapers.py` (gradients and soft shapes, a seeded dither; CC0, `assets/wallpapers/LICENSE-Wallpapers.txt`), and pictures copied from a USB drive (below). The Wallpaper picker (`WallpaperPicker.qml`): a modal 4-column grid of 320 px thumbnails (Default, bundled, copies), the current one badged, "Add from USB drive..." while a mounted removable drive has pictures, "Delete" for the highlighted copy.

### 3.6 Glass and blur

Glass is translucent white over a **Gaussian blur** of what is behind it (`MultiEffect`, `blurMax` per tier: High 64, Balanced 32, Performance 0). Rules: glass always comes with blur, or it turns opaque (`glassOpaque`): never translucent glass over sharp art. One shared blurred copy of L0 (downscaled), sampled by every glass panel; never a second fullscreen blur. `glassStrong` (built, T-146: white 12 %, with a saturation boost of 0.3 on the blur) is for the top bar and the quick menu. As built, each glass pill (`GlassPill.qml`: section nav, status cluster) blurs its own quarter-resolution crop of L0 through a rounded mask, not one shared texture; both are small, never fullscreen. **Layers (built, T-204):** on top of the fill every glass surface carries `GlassLayers.qml`: the optional `glassTint`, the top sheen (`glassSheen` over `glassSheenSpan` of the height), the grain tile (`assets/glass-grain.png`, 128x128, tiled, scaled by `glassGrainOpacity`; made by `scripts/make-grain.py` from a fixed pseudo-random sequence, white 2 % and black 2.4 % alpha noise, from no third-party source, so it carries no licence) and the lit rim (`GlassRim.qml`: one 1 px ring, a `Shape` with a diagonal gradient from `glassRimLit` to `glassRim`, replacing the flat `glassBorder` hairline on glass). Used by `GlassPill`, the Guide panel, toasts and the Settings rows (`SettingControl`: rim hidden while the focus ring shows, no grain since nothing is blurred behind a row). Tiers: sheen, rim and tint everywhere, also on `glassOpaque`; grain only with blur (High, Balanced: `Theme.grainOn`); `GlassLayers` exposes `sheenVisible`, `rimVisible`, `tintVisible`, `grainVisible`. Nothing uses `tint` yet: the Guide panel and the dialogs are opaque (`panel`), and the translucent sheets come with the quick menu (T-147) and B-003. **Contrast** of `text` (#f2f4f8) on tinted glass, computed with the sheen included at its 6 % top edge: over pure white art 2.5:1 (2.7:1 without the sheen), over 90 % grey 2.9:1, over 75 % grey 3.6:1, over mid grey 5.6:1; the same glass without the tint over pure white art is 0.9:1. **Plainly:** the 44 % `tint` gives only 2.5:1 for white text over pure white art, below the 4.5:1 that §7 asks for, so `tint` must not carry text until the lead decides (Inbox, design). **Per theme (T-209):** this is about the dark themes' `glassTint` (dark neutral at 44 to 50 %). Daylight's `glassTint` is white at 55 %, which carries dark text at 5.5:1 or more over pure white and pure black (in the gate); `tst_theme_contrast.qml` pins the Dreamglass number so it cannot change silently. The untinted glass pills (`glassStrong`) over near-white hero art are 0.9:1 for white text; that is pre-existing and not changed by T-204. Glass over a **running app** blurs a downscaled copy of the app surface; until that is measured on a GPU (B-003) the quick menu uses `glassOpaque` on every tier.

### 3.8 Material and motion v2 (decided, lead, 2026-11-06; built by T-204 and T-205 except the edge accent)

Owner, 2026-11-06: "I like the animations and the glass effect" of an open-source native TV app for LG webOS (GPL-3.0, written in C on SDL2/OpenGL). It cannot be ported (C and raw GL, not QML), and no code or assets are taken: its *techniques and parameters* were studied and are re-built here in Qt Quick on our tokens (its glass look credits the MIT `liquid-glass-react`; credit as inspiration, never its name or brand in the UI). What we adopt, with the numbers (px at the 1080p design unit):

### 3.7 Icons (decided ADR-013; built, T-157)

- **Set:** Material Symbols Rounded (Apache-2.0), a subset font in the Qt resources (`shell/fonts/`), family names `Theme.iconFamily` (outlined) and `Theme.iconFamilyFilled`, registered by `main.cpp` (and the test host) with `QFontDatabase::addApplicationFont`. The licence ships next to the fonts (`LICENSE-MaterialSymbols.txt`) and in the image (`/usr/share/licenses/ruyaos/`); the About pane's licence line names the icon font.
- **Drawing:** `Icon.qml` by name (`Icon { name: "settings" }`): a name → code point table inside the component, never ligatures and never a raw code point in a page; an unknown name draws nothing and warns once. The table is `Icon.codePoints`; `shell/fonts/icons.txt` lists the same pairs and feeds `scripts/subset-icons.sh`, and `scripts/lint-icons.ps1` (in `dev.ps1 shell` and CI) fails if they differ. Adding an icon = one line in `icons.txt`, the same pair in the table, then re-running the subset script in WSL (`docs/dev-setup.md`). As built: `Icon` takes `u`, `size` (default `Theme.iconM`), `color` (default `text`) and `filled`, and is `Accessible.ignored`.
- **Style:** weight 400, grade 0, optical size 40: two static cuts of the variable font, outlined (FILL 0) normally and filled (FILL 1) for the current or selected state (static cuts render the same on every Qt ≥ 6.8; no variable-axis API needed). **Overlaps are merged when the cuts are made** (`--remove-overlaps`, T-206): the variable font draws a filled glyph as coincident contours of opposite direction that cancel in the nonzero fill rule, and the Windows rasteriser left the active Settings gear speckled with holes; `scripts/check-font-coverage.py` (in `dev.ps1 test`/`shell` and CI) fails when a cut has a glyph with two contours of the same points. The nav gear is `iconM` in both states and `onPrimary` on `primaryFill` (about 18:1) when active; `tst_icon.qml` asserts the same pixel size at 1080p and 720p and a contrast of at least 4.5:1. Colour from the text tokens (`text`, `onPrimary`, `textDim`, the toast kind colours), never a category tint.
- **Glyphs in the subset (T-223):** `settings`, `volume_up`, `volume_off`; the battery: `battery_full`, `battery_alert`, `battery_charging_full` and `battery_0_bar` ... `battery_5_bar`; the device kinds: `sports_esports` (controller), `headphones` (headset), `speaker`, `mouse`, `keyboard`, `smartphone` (phone, T-199) and `bluetooth` (anything else); and three glyphs of our own, `pad_playstation`, `pad_xbox` and `pad_switch` (see **Device and controller family icons**). `scripts/check-font-coverage.py` also fails when a code point of `icons.txt` has no glyph in either cut. **Battery level mapping** (`BatteryChip.levelIconFor`, seven steps by percentage): 0-7 `battery_0_bar`, 8-21 `battery_1_bar`, 22-35 `battery_2_bar`, 36-50 `battery_3_bar`, 51-64 `battery_4_bar`, 65-78 `battery_5_bar`, 79-100 `battery_full`; `battery_charging_full` (the bolt) while charging whatever the level, `battery_alert` in the warning colour while the device is discharging at 15 % or less, `battery_full` when the device reports itself full. Material's `battery_6_bar` is not used (the top step is the full battery). The glyphs are vertical batteries.
- **Device and controller family icons (T-199).** `DeviceIcons.qml` maps a device's `kind` and `family` to an icon name and to the kind in words (the Bluetooth pane's rows, the status cluster's chips, the device toasts and a screen reader all ask it); a kind it does not know is `bluetooth` and the word "Bluetooth device", a controller with no or an unknown family is `sports_esports`. **Drawn:** `pad_playstation` (two grips that splay out, a touchpad bar, both sticks low and level, D-pad left and four buttons right), `pad_xbox` (a stick top left, the D-pad low in the middle, four buttons top right, the second stick low right, a round button on top) and `pad_switch` (a landscape body with a screen between two rails, stick over D-pad on the left, buttons over stick on the right), in the line style of Material's `sports_esports` (a rounded body, sticks and buttons as discs, a cross for the D-pad; outlined, and solid with the details cut out in the filled cut). They are **layouts, not logos**: no vendor mark, no wordmark and no button symbols (the four buttons are plain dots). They are made by `scripts/add-pad-glyphs.py` (rounded polygons, discs and unions with skia-pathops; run by `scripts/subset-icons.sh` for both cuts) and live at the private-use code points `U+E005`-`U+E007`, which the Material font does not use (the script fails if upstream ever does); `icons.txt` lists them with the word `ruya` after the code point, `lint-icons.ps1` accepts that and `check-font-coverage.py` checks the glyphs. **Deliberately generic:** Material's gamepad for every pad whose maker we cannot tell (8BitDo, generic HID pads, a pad over USB without a family), and for every controller in the status cluster (`Batteries` carries no family); headsets, speakers, keyboards, mice and phones are Material's own glyphs. Real vendor art (logos, the PlayStation button symbols) is an owner decision with a licence answer (ADR-level), not a builder's.
- **Sizes:** `iconM` 40 (pills, rows, toasts, OSD), `iconL` 64 (empty states). An icon in a pill or button is centred like its label would be.
- **Controller glyphs** are not icons: `ButtonGlyph.qml` draws the button's letter (A, B, X, Y, LB, RB, Start) in `typeMicro` on a `primaryFill` circle (`glyphSize` 40) with `onPrimary` text (hint bar, T-158).

## 4. Focus

| Item kind | Focused look |
|---|---|
| Cards (tile, profile, later app art) | `focusScale` 1.08, white ring `focusRingWidth` at `focusRingOffset` outside, ring radius = shape radius + offset, z above neighbours |
| Rows and buttons | No scale; fill `focusFill`, border 4 px `focusRing`; unfocused, a glass row shows the lit rim (`GlassLayers`, §3.6) where it used to show a 1 px `glassBorder` (buttons and keys still have the `glassBorder` hairline), and the rim is hidden while the ring shows |
| Keys (PIN pad, keyboard) | Fill only (`keyFocusFill` / `entryKeyFocusFill`) plus the ring; the grid never moves |
| List selection without focus | `selectedFill`, text `text` and DemiBold |

The gliding highlight (§3.8) is a fill under the ring, never a replacement for it: the ring sits on the focused item and shows at once while the highlight is still gliding. The ring never fades out during a move (it may fade in), is never hidden by a quality tier, and focus is never shown by colour alone. **Ring colour by theme (T-209):** white on the dark themes, a dark navy (`#0f1420`) on Daylight, where white would vanish on frosted white glass; its width and offset are the same everywhere, and `tst_theme_contrast.qml` holds it to 3:1 against the glass, the ground, the panel and the scrims of each theme. The task's idea of a 2 px opposite-lightness halo around the ring is **not built**: it needs one shared ring component (the ring is drawn by 18 separate `border` rectangles) and no surface of the five themes needs it today; it is an Inbox item for the day a theme or a wallpaper puts the ring over art of the ring's own lightness. Unavailable items use `disabledOpacity` (0.4) and are skipped or say why ("Not available").

### 5.1 Full-screen apps (look; behaviour in `02-shell.md` §1.5)

## 6. Writing

- Sentence case everywhere ("Reduced motion", not "Reduced Motion"); the brand "RuyaOS" and language names are never translated.
- Buttons and actions are verbs ("Restart", "Add profile"); a confirm repeats the verb of the question ("Restart now?" → Restart / Cancel).
- Errors say what happened and what the user can do, in at most two short sentences ("Wrong PIN. 2 tries left."). No "please", no codes, no blame.
- Row labels stay under about 28 characters in English so the Turkish fits.

## 7. Accessibility

**Contrast gate (T-209, `tst_theme_contrast.qml`).** Every built-in theme is measured with the shell's own arithmetic (`ThemeMath`) and must reach: `text` >= 4.5:1 on the ground, `panel`, `glassOpaque`, the scrims, the hero and caption washes over pure white and pure black art (at their strongest), toasts, the primary and danger buttons, and on `glass`/`glassStrong` over the Backdrop (the theme's `defaultTint`: each blob over the gradient's brighter end, and the plain ground); `textDim` >= 4.5:1 on the opaque surfaces only (ground, `panel`, `glassOpaque`); `errorText`/`warningText` and the badge colours >= 4.5:1 on `panel`; the toast icon colours and the glass text where the two big blobs overlap >= 3:1 (large text); the label of a Settings **peek row** (drawn at `peekOpacity`, text and glass both at that alpha over the backdrop) >= 3:1 over the gradient and each blob (row labels are 30 px body text, large at the 1080p design size) and >= 2:1 where the blobs overlap (not 3:1: the bright dark themes, Aurora worst, give 2.3:1 there at 0.7; the row is whole once focused); the focus ring >= 3:1 on the glass, the ground, the panel and the scrims. A light theme additionally holds its glass text over pure white and pure black ("any wallpaper"). A deliberately bad fixture proves the gate fails. **Not promised, and pinned in the same file so a fix must change this text:** the four dark themes' glass over a bright wallpaper or bright hero art (white text on untinted glass over white is 0.9:1; the wallpaper needs dimming, T-208), `glassTint` carrying text on a dark theme (2.5:1 over white art, §3.6), the white blob overlap on a bright avatar tint (Dreamglass: 3.6:1 at the default tint, which the gate holds to its floor of 3; over the orange, cyan and green avatar colours the same overlap is 2.5 to 2.8:1; the default tint is gated, the profile palette is not), and the initial letter on an avatar colour (`text` on the orange, cyan and green avatars is 2.2, 2.5 and 2.9:1 on the dark themes; on Daylight the dark initial is 3.1:1 on the blue-grey guest avatar and 3.2:1 on the blue one, the lowest of the palette, still at the large-text floor). All four are Inbox items for the lead.

- **Informative graphics** (chart bars, usage bars) on glass need 3:1 against the row they sit on, worst case (backdrop at its brightest tint): `text` at reduced opacity or `primaryFill`, not `textDim` (about 2.3:1 on a bright tint). Check it in the screenshot of **every theme** (§3.1), not only Dreamglass. T-247 measured Dreamglass only (pixel samples of `90-activity-week-en`: 4.6:1 over the dark part of the row, 3.2:1 over the brightest); Aurora, Ember, Daylight and the Performance look were **not** checked, and the contrast gate (`tst_theme_contrast.qml`) has no row for chart bars yet.
- **A chart is read-only.** Its values are read bar by bar with Left/Right while its row has focus (a text readout above the plot), and the whole data is also exposed as one accessible text.
- Text contrast ≥ 4.5:1 against the surface it actually sits on, worst case (backdrop at its brightest tint); the focus ring ≥ 3:1 against both the item and its surroundings (white on any tint passes).
- High-contrast theme, text scale and focus-ring size (`02-shell.md` §7) will be token sets over the same names, so components need no change: never compute a colour in a component (`Qt.lighter` of a token for a gradient is the one allowed exception, as in `Backdrop.qml`).
- Every interactive item has `Accessible.name`; decoration has none. Done in T-144 and checked by `tst_accessibility.qml`, which walks the focus chain of the picker, the PIN pad, Home, every Settings pane, the Guide overlay, Games (grid, filter, empty page), Store (toolbar, rows, results), the Store keyboard, an app's detail page, the power confirmation buttons, the first-run wizard including its name-and-PIN step (T-176, T-186) and the Settings -> Profiles add and manage flows (T-186), and asserts a non-empty `Accessible.name` on each focused item. The Confirm button of `PowerConfirm` shows the action as its label and the accessible name is that same text (WCAG 2.5.3; T-186 named it, T-194 made the label match): "Suspend now", "Restart now", "Power off now", and for `custom` the caller's `customConfirmName` ("Delete Ada", "Roll back now"); only a custom dialog without a name falls back to "Confirm". Cancel stays "Cancel". The on-screen keyboard (`TextEntry`) has an `Accessible.description` that says what Back does ("Press Back to delete, Back on an empty field to cancel", or "Press Back to cancel" when the field is empty or after typing on a physical keyboard) and the hint bar shows `B Delete` while a controller's Back would delete a letter, `B Cancel` otherwise (T-194). The wizard's key sink is named after the step ("Welcome to RuyaOS", "Network", "You're all set"); the other steps focus their own rows. Lists that hold focus (a ListView or GridView whose delegates are not items of their own) name themselves after the current delegate; setting rows read `label, value` (`SettingControl.accessibleValue`), toggles are `CheckBox` with `checked`, choice rows `ComboBox`, sliders `Slider`; buttons, tiles, profile cards ("Name, PIN protected" when a PIN is set), category items, PIN keys and keyboard keys are `Button`/`ListItem` with their visible text.

## 8. Changing the design system

- **Builders use what exists.** Pick the closest token and component. If the task truly needs a new value or component, use the closest existing one, add an Inbox line `(T-nnn) design: needs <token/component> because <reason>`, and say so in the Outcome. Never add a one-off literal (`// token-ok` is for non-visual numbers only).
- **The lead (or a task that says so) changes the system.** A new or changed token or component is one commit that updates `Theme.qml`, this file (table and status), the screenshots that changed, and names the canvas board to update. Mark the commit `design:` in its summary (area `shell` or `docs`).
- **Owner-level:** a new typeface or icon set (licence), a new brand colour, or anything that changes the five principles; ask, as a *Proposed* ADR.
- **Screenshot review** (every UI task, `dev.ps1 shots`): focus visible on every screen; nothing outside the safe area; type only from §3.2; `textDim` only on opaque surfaces; Turkish fits without clipping; Performance tier (`--effects performance`) keeps the layout.
