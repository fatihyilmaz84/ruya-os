# RuyaOS — Shell UI/UX

Status: **Draft v1** · Parent: [00-overview](00-overview.md)

The shell (`ruya-shell`) is the only UI the user sees. It's written in Qt 6 Quick/QML with C++ glue. Per ADR-002 (option A, accepted) it is also the Wayland compositor, directly on KMS (T-130; on the Windows host and in the tests it is an ordinary window). It holds no business logic: state and side effects live in the Rust services, which it reaches over D-Bus.

### 1.1 Page model (as built, T-101)
- Home's section nav carries stable keys: `home`, `games`, `media`, `store`, `settings` (`SectionNav.keys`). Accept on a pill (or LB/RB) calls `Shell.openPage(key)` (`home` just focuses Play); a page key `power` exists too, reached from the Guide overlay's own choices and by code; a page opens a detail page on top of itself with `Shell.openPage(key, params)`. `pageStack` holds `{key, params}` entries; `currentPage` is the top key, `currentParams` its params (also the page's `params` property). `closePage()` pops the top entry.
- A page extends `Page.qml`: title below the header, a content area inside the safe area, and Back (`Escape`/`Back`), which plays `Sfx.back()` and closes the page. The page's `focus: true` element gets focus when it opens. Every stack entry has its own live page item inside a slot item under `pageHost`: only the top slot is shown and enabled, lower ones stay alive hidden and disabled, so their scroll position, state and focused item are kept (the shell notes the focused item when a page is pushed over and restores it on pop). A popped slot fades out, then is destroyed once the page on top has finished its fade and slide and that frame is on screen (T-349: a section switch, e.g. Store to Settings, tears the Store page down after the Settings glide, not in its last frame). A window that showed no frame while the slot closed (it is not presenting: the display is asleep, so `update()` brings no frame either) has no glide on screen to protect, and the slot is destroyed as soon as the page on top has settled (T-460). Signing out destroys all of them at once. Pages not in the shell's own list can be registered through `Shell.extraPages` (key -> Component).
- Transition: home fades out and slides left, the page fades in and slides in from the right, over `Theme.pageMs` (`Theme.pageSlide` px); reversed on close. Every page open writes one line to the log (T-349, `PageProbe`): `ruya-shell: page-open <key> create=<ms> firstSwap=<ms> afterAnim=<ms> frames=<n> maxFrame=<ms>`, measured from the key press (`openSection`/`openPage`): the page exists (`create`), the first frame after it is shown (`firstSwap`), the slide and fade have ended (`afterAnim`), the frames shown until then and the longest gap between two of them (`maxFrame`, the worst frame of the transition, the key press counting as the first). A page closed or replaced before it settles writes nothing. A nested page uses the same fade/slide, cross-fading with the page below. The backdrop stays. While a page is open, home is disabled, so arrow keys cannot reach Home.
- Section nav on pages (T-168): one `SectionNav` instance in the shell's top layer (`navShown`: signed in, no app, and either Home or a section page on top; fades with `pageMs`). Its `section` follows the page on screen (`navSection`), so the Settings gear is filled on Settings. `Page.qml` turns an Up that no content item accepted into `upFromTop` (the shell focuses the nav on the page's pill), and PageUp/PageDown into `stepRequested`; Down from the nav restores the page's saved focus; Back from the nav closes the page. Moving between pills moves one gliding highlight under them (T-205, 04 §3.8): a picture only, focus and the nav's accessible name change at once and stay on the nav. Switching section from a page closes the open pages first, so Back lands on Home.
- Closing a page (or the quick menu) restores focus to the Home item that had it (a nav pill, Play, Details or the Recent row at its card): `Shell.homeSavedFocus`, set in `openPage` and when the overlay opens, used once by `focusHome()`. `Shell.routeFocus()`/`focusCurrent()` is the single place that decides where focus goes.
- The quick menu (§1.4) opens from home or from any page (the shell handles `HomePage` and `Super` when nothing under focus does; `Menu` is the context-menu key, not Guide). Closing it returns focus to the page. "Switch profile" clears the page stack, so the next sign-in lands on home.
- Games, Media and Store are placeholders ("Coming soon" plus one focusable element) until their tasks land; Settings (§1.2) and Power (§1.4) are real.

### 1.5 Full-screen apps (as built; owner 2026-10-28; T-130, T-138, T-147)
- **Whose window (T-164, ADR-023):** `Shell.addApp(item, profile)` records the profile the app runs as (the compositor maps each Wayland client to it by peer uid; "" is the shell's own). A window is shown only while someone is signed in and only to its own profile: locking hides every app (the picker and the wizard are never covered) and signing in again as the same profile shows its apps with the focus on the newest; another profile does not see them, and a hidden app gets no focus and no keys (`focusApp` refuses it). `shownApps` lists what is visible; `appOpen` means one is. What a profile switch does to the hidden apps (freeze, stop) is T-165's.
- Every app and game runs full screen in the shell's app layer (ADR-002 A, T-130). As built (T-150): `Shell.addApp(item, profile)`, `focusApp`, `removeApp` manage the layer (`appLayer`, z 5: above home and pages, below the launch splash (z 6), toasts, modals, the quick menu and the volume OSD); the last app item removed or destroyed gives focus back to the page or Home. A window names its Flatpak application id (`item.appId`; the compositor's surface items have it, `Compositor.qml`), which maps it to its `Session.running` record, its library item and its card; a window without one (a test client) is matched to the newest running app of the profile. **An id that arrives after the window does too (T-435):** Electron sets the xdg toplevel's `app_id` right after the toplevel exists, so the compositor hands the window over with an empty one; the record takes the id when the item's `appId` changes (`Shell.appIdArrived`), so Resume from the Running card, "Switch to" and the quick menu find the window (before, Accept on ACBR's card launched again and said "ACBR is already running"). **A window goes by its process first (T-459):** the app id a window gives itself is not always its Flatpak id (a pygame game says `python3.12`, Superfluous Returnz `superfluous-returnz`), so the compositor gives each surface item its client's pid (Wayland credentials, `client.processId`) and `addApp` asks `Session.instanceOfPid`; the record takes the answered instance (`instanceArrived`), and `appIdOf(record)` is that instance's app id, the window's own only as a fallback. `windowOf` (Resume, Play on a running game), `runningFor` (the Hub's title and art, the instance Close game stops and HubPause freezes), "Switch to" and Home's Running card all go by it; `runningFor` falls back to the window's own app id (then the Hub shows that id's title), and to the profile's newest app only for a window that names none; **one binding for all of it (T-467):** `Shell.instanceOfWindow(record)` is the running instance a window belongs to: its process's, else the one running the app id the window gives itself *in the window's profile* (two profiles running the same app never cross, and `appFrozen` reads the window's own instance), else the profile's one running app that no other window claims (the same unambiguous rule as Close game below), so a game whose window says `python3.12`, nothing, or whose process Session1 could not map is still found by `appIdOf`, `windowOf`, `appFrozen`, `runningFor`, Resume, Play on its card and "Switch to"; Close game goes by the window's instance, else its own app id's running app; for a window that names no running app (Session1 could not tell) it takes the profile's one running app that no other window shown to the profile claims (by instance or app id), and only when that is unambiguous (exactly one such app, and no other window that names none); while the window's InstanceOfPid answer is on its way it waits for it (at most 2 s). An answer naming an instance of another profile than the window's is ignored; a failed one is logged. A window that turns out to be the app being launched ends the splash when its instance arrives. Not covered: X11-only games (XWayland is not offered).
- **Launch (T-147).** Play (the Home hero, a Recent card, a Games card; Accept on a card that is already running comes back to the app instead, see Home below) calls `Shell.launchApp(appId, title, picture)`: the launch splash (`LaunchSplash.qml`, 04 §5.1) comes up over everything but the quick menu and toasts, holds the focus and takes every key except Guide, and `Session.launch(appId, <signed-in profile>)` is asked. One launch at a time: a second Play while one is pending is ignored. The splash shows the title at once and "Starting…" after Session1 answered `ok`. It ends, with a cross-fade to the app, when the window of that app arrives (`addApp`: its `appId` is the launch's, or empty), and the app gets the focus. A failure status (anything but `ok` and `already-running`) or `AppExited` for the launch (`launch-failed`, `crashed`, even a plain quit) closes the splash, puts the focus back where Play was (or on Home when that is gone) and, for a failure, shows the error toast "<title> could not be started. Try again." (a crash: "<title> stopped unexpectedly. Try again."; the title is the request's own, capped to 30 characters, never the raw launch spec). `already-running` brings the app to the front, or, when it has no window yet, shows "<title> is already running". After `launchStillMs` (20 s) without a window the splash says "Still starting…" and offers **Close** (it takes the focus; A on it, or B, stops the instance and returns to where Play was, silently; the hint bar shows `A` Close and `B` Close). Guide still opens the quick menu (without an app part) during a launch; Back returns to the splash. Signing out cancels the launch. A game with no Session1 launch path (a Steam game: `Library.sessionAppId` is empty) shows no Play button on the hero (Details takes the focus), Accept on its Recent card or Games card opens Details, and no toast is raised; nothing says "Starting" before Session1 has answered (the splash is the only feedback, there is no "Starting…" toast).
- **Quick menu over an app (T-147).** Guide (short press) opens it over the live app: header with the app's art, title and "Running for 12 min" ("Just started" in the first minute), then **Resume** (focus first), **Home**, **Close game**, one **Switch to <app>** per other running app, **Volume**, **Switch profile**, **Power** (§1.4). Back, Guide or Resume closes it and returns the focus to the app, and no key reaches the app while it is open. The hint bar says `A` Select, `B` Resume (the check for the open menu comes before the one for an app in front, so the app's silent bar does not hide it). **Home** leaves the app running: it loses the focus, any open page is closed, Home is in front with the focus on the app's Recent card (an app that is not among the recent ones is put first in the row) showing a **Running** pill, and the hero's Play reads "Resume". Accept on that card (or on the hero's Resume) puts the same window back in front with no new launch. **Close game** asks `Session.stop(instance)` and shows the **closing screen** (T-458): the launch splash in its closing mode over Home (the game's art, its title and the line "Closing <title>…" in place of the loading line, the same dots), which holds the focus and takes every key except Guide (Back, Accept and the arrows do nothing, so the player cannot be stranded on it; Guide opens the Hub over it and Back returns to it); the hint bar is empty. It ends on `AppExited` for that instance, or when Stop answers that nothing runs (or refuses), with Home in front and the focus where Close left it (the game's Recent card). If the game has not ended after 5 s (`Shell.closeSplashMs`) Home comes back anyway and the game's card shows a neutral **Closing** pill instead of Running, the hero's Play reads "Closing…", and Accept on the card, on Play or any other launch path only toasts "<title> is still closing." (`info`) until `AppExited`; the pill then becomes nothing. A game the service reports `killed` after its grace needs no message; if it is still listed `killCheckMs` (1 s) after the kill the shell shows a `warning` toast "<title> did not close. Restart the console if it keeps running." and the card says Running again. Reduced motion: the screen is a cut, the dots stand still, the text only. A failed stop shows "The game could not be closed. Try again." (`error`) and ends the screen. With no instance (none matches the window, or the fallback is ambiguous, T-459) nothing is stopped: a `warning` toast says "Could not close <title>." ("Could not close the running app." when the window names nothing) and the Hub stays open with the focus on Close game, never a silent Home with the game still running. **Switch to** raises the other app's window and focuses it (thawing it if the Hub froze it). **Every running app is reachable (T-467):** the Hub lists a **Switch to <app>** row for every app in `Session.running` of the signed-in profile except the one in front (frozen or not, with a window or not), also when opened from Home, where these rows come first and the focus starts on the first (an app behind Home with its window found is one Accept from the front). A row for a running app whose window has not come reads "Switch to <app> (no window yet)"; Accept toasts "<app> has no window yet." (`info`) and the Hub stays. Play or Accept on a running app's card with a window brings that window back, thawed; only an app with no window at all gets the "is already running" toast. Holding Guide still opens the power choices. The volume OSD stays away while the menu is open (its Volume row shows the level).
- While an app (or the splash) is in front only the quick menu, the volume OSD and toasts of kind `error` and `warning` are drawn; every other toast waits in the queue (one that was already on screen goes back to its head) until the shell is in front again (`Notifications.appInFront`). Apps left behind Home count as running: `appRunning` stays true, so the nightly update restart (T-154) still waits (`Update.noteActivity`). One app is in front; others keep running until closed (memory limits: later).
- **Reduced motion:** `launchMs` is 0, so the splash appears and goes as a cut, the dots stand still, and the quick menu's slide is a cut too.

### 1.6 Ruya Hub (target; ADR-033, designed T-447, lead 2026-11-10; built: the Quick Hub, T-448, rebuilt as layout A by T-471 (owner, 2026-11-12): status row, game card, Home | Close game, running strip, Volume, panel icons; the panel mechanism and the Audio panel, T-450; the Performance panel, T-452; the Controllers panel, T-453; not yet: Notifications, T-245)

Ruya Hub is the quick menu grown into the in-game control centre. It has three levels: the **Quick Hub** (what Guide opens), **panels** (one level deeper, still over the game) and the **full Ruya interface** (Settings, Library: choosing one leaves the Hub). It never becomes a second desktop: anything that needs more than one panel's worth of rows lives in Settings and the Hub links to it.

**Layout.** The Hub stays the 640 u panel on the left over `scrimOverlay` (04 §5, Quick menu), so the game stays visible on the right two thirds. A panel **replaces the Quick Hub's content in the same panel** (no second column): one column keeps focus simple (Up/Down between rows, Left/Right only inside a row of several controls) and keeps the game in view. The panel slides in over `overlayMs`; content changes cross-fade over `focusMs` with a `pageSlide`-sized shift; reduced motion: cuts. Readable at 10 ft on a 720p TV (sizes in `u`).

**The Quick Hub, top to bottom (over an app; layout A, owner 2026-11-12, built T-471).** One screen, no scroll, at most five focus stops: the Hub is an interruption (get back to the game, leave or switch it, change what matters mid-game); everything else is one Home press away.
1. **Status row** (not focusable): the clock, the network (icon and name, or "Not connected"), the muted microphone (`mic_off`, while the Audio panel's Mute microphone is on and a microphone exists) and up to two device battery chips with a "+N" for the rest (the same `BatteryChip` and `TimeZone` clock as the top-right cluster, not that cluster itself).
2. **Game card = Resume** (component "Hub game card", 04 §5): the game's art (`AppArt`, 256 u, larger than the old header's 200 u), its title and its state: "Paused · 12 min" while Session1 reports it frozen ("Paused" under a minute), else "Running for 12 min" / "Just started". Focus lands here on every open; A resumes (there is no separate Resume button).
3. **Home | Close game:** one row of two pills, Left/Right between them. Home sends the apps behind Home (§1.5); Close game keeps its behaviour and its closing screen (§1.5, T-458).
4. **Running strip** (component "Running strip", 04 §5): one art tile per *other* running app of the profile (T-467: frozen or not, with or without a window yet), its Library card art (the tile picture, else the hero; the initial on a tint without one), the name under the focused tile only; Left/Right along the strip; A switches to it (Switch to: the chosen app is thawed, the old one stays frozen; an app with no window yet says so in a toast and the Hub stays). Absent when nothing else runs.
5. **Volume** row: Left/Right by 5, A mutes; muted, the row says "Muted".
6. **Panel icons** (component "Hub icon row", 04 §5): one row of icon buttons, Left/Right between them, the focused one's name under it; A opens the panel. In order: **Audio**, **Controllers**, **Performance**, **Power** (Notifications joins when T-245 builds it). An icon appears only when its panel is built; nothing is shown disabled.

A row of several controls is one focus stop: Left/Right move inside it and stop at its ends, Up/Down keep each row's column while the Hub is open (one exception: Up from the running strip into Home | Close game picks the column nearest to the tile it left, a tile in the middle slot going to Close game), and every open starts each row on its first control. **Without an app** (the Hub over Home or a page): the status row, the running strip (if anything runs behind Home), Volume and the panel icons; the focus starts on the first of these that exists (the strip, else Volume); B closes. At the default UI size the full Hub fits the panel at 720p and 1080p (`tst_quickmenu` checks both); at a larger UI size (RUY-401) the game card's art and the strip's tiles shrink (down to 35 %; the text, the pills and the focus rings keep the UI size) until the rows fit: the Hub never scrolls at 100 % and 125 %; at 150 % and above it fits while it can (a game alone at 150 %) and may fall back to scrolling as a long panel does, with the art at its floor (a game with two other apps at 150 % on a 1080p screen; lead decision, 2026-11-12). The line for the focused item's name under the strip and under the icon row takes no height while that row has no focus (no gap) and opens over `focusMs` when the focus enters; the art is fitted with it open, so it does not change with the focus. Up from the strip into Home | Close game lands on the column nearest to the tile it left (the leftmost tile on Home, the rightmost on Close game); the hint bar over the game card lists `A Resume` only (B resumes too).

**Removed from the Hub (owner, 2026-11-12, T-471; each stays in Settings or Home):** the Recent rows (Home is one press away; launching a new game mid-game is rare), the Wi-Fi and Bluetooth tiles (turning them off mid-game drops an online session or every Bluetooth pad; Settings keeps them), the Mute tile (A on Volume mutes) and the Mute mic tile (the Audio panel's Mute microphone row; the status row shows the muted glyph), the Display panel (a mode change restarts the console, never mid-game; Settings > Display & motion keeps the modes, and since T-471 also the Brightness row, which before lived only in this panel), the Switch profile row (the Power panel's last choice), the "System" heading with the image version (the always-on version tag and About keep the version) and the Close row without an app (B closes).

**Tiles** (T-448, T-450; retired by T-471): the Quick Hub has no quick tiles any more (see Removed above); `QuickTile.qml` is gone. **Panels.** Each has a title row (`typeTitle`, not focusable) and its rows; focus lands on the first row (the Performance panel's overlay switch).
- **Audio** (T-450, built): Volume (the Hub's volume row: Left/Right by 5, Accept mutes), Mute (a toggle row, value On/Off), Output (a choice row over `Audio.outputs`: the value is the current output and Left/Right or Accept make the next or previous *usable* one the default through `SetOutput`, in the service's order and wrapping; an output whose route says it is not connected is not offered; the row changes with the service's answer, and a refusal toasts "Could not switch the audio output."), Microphone (a choice row over `Audio.inputs`, the same way, toast "Could not switch the microphone.") and Mute microphone (a toggle row), the last two only while `Audio.inputs` is not empty (T-449). A choice that asks the service (`async`: the output, the microphone) changes its row only with the answer; presses that come before the answer step on from the **pending target** of the last press (kept until `Set*Finished` and the list show it; a refusal drops it), not from the old default. `openPanel(id)` puts the focus on the first row; Back (`closePanel()`) returns to the Hub with the focus on the panel's icon; Guide closes the whole Hub; opening the Hub always starts in the Hub. The rows cross-fade over `focusMs` and slide by `pageSlide` (`swap`, a cut under reduced motion). A new panel is one entry in `panelIds`, one table in `panelDef` and a glyph in `iconOf`.
- **Controllers** (T-453, built): one read-only row per connected pad, then **Controller settings**. A pad row is an *info* row with a glyph: the family icon (`DeviceIcons.iconFor`: PlayStation, Xbox, Switch silhouettes, else the generic gamepad) before two lines, the name above and "Battery 62 %, charging · Bluetooth" under it ("Battery 62 %", "Battery full", "Battery not reported"; "USB" or "Bluetooth"); the whole line is in `text` (04 §7), the words carry the state. **Where the pads come from:** the service's list, `Bluetooth.gamepads` (`Devices1.Bluetooth.ListGamepads`, `03`): every pad present now, wired ones included, with the service's own `transport` ("USB", "Bluetooth"; any other: no connection word). The shell asks at start, when the service appears and after every `GamepadConnected` / `GamepadDisconnected`. The battery is looked up per pad: for a Bluetooth pad by address in `Batteries.devices` (UPower), else the paired device's own level (`Bluetooth.devices`); for a wired pad, the one address-less controller battery of its family when that match is unambiguous (one such pad, one such battery); no reading at all is "Battery not reported" (a wired Xbox or generic pad is a pad with no battery). A Bluetooth pad takes its alias from `Bluetooth.devices` (`DeviceIcons.shownName`); a pad the Bluetooth list does not know is named as the kernel names it. Rows are sorted by name (then id) and keyed by the pad's id, so a battery change updates a row in place and never moves it; two Bluetooth pads of one name get the end of their address ("Wireless Controller (00:04)"). The list follows the service live: a pad that goes while the panel is open leaves it, and the focus stays in the list (on the next row, or the same row if it was another); a pad that comes appears without moving the focus. **Controller settings** is an *action* row (new `kind`, hint `A` Open): `settingsRequested("bluetooth")`, which `Shell.openSettingsFromHub` turns into Home's route (the apps keep running behind it; level 3 of ADR-033 leaves the Hub) and the Settings page on Bluetooth & controllers; Back from it lands on Home. No mapping, profile or player controls until Ruya Input (T-366..T-374). None connected: one line "No controllers connected." (an info row with no value, body size), then Controller settings. Focus lands on the first pad (or the one line); Back returns to the Hub on the Controllers entry.
- **Performance** (T-452, reshaped by T-469, built): a switch for the numbers and the interval, nothing to read. The Hub freezes the game (T-457), so a reading taken in the Hub is a paused game's (owner, 2026-11-11); the numbers are drawn over the running game instead. Two rows: **Performance overlay** (a toggle row, On/Off, Accept toggles; stored **per profile**: `DeviceSettings.performanceOverlayProfiles`, Settings keys `hub/performanceOverlay/<profile id>`, off by default, survives a restart; another profile's switch is not touched) and **Update every** (a choice row: 0.5, 1, 2, 5 s; Left/Right or Accept; `DeviceSettings.performanceIntervalMs`, which the polling follows at once). Focus lands on the switch, Back returns to the Hub on the Performance entry. The panel itself asks for nothing: no polling, no frame counting. **The overlay** (`PerformanceReadout.qml`, in `Shell.qml` at z 7, `perfOverlay`): shown while the switch is on for the signed-in profile and a game is in front (`appOpen` with a focused app: not Home, not a page) and none of these covers it: the Hub, the launch or closing splash, the screen saver or the dark screen; a toast stack hides the right block only (the stack is in the top right corner; the left block stays). It is two small blocks of plain text on an opaque `panel` scrim inside the safe area: top left **FPS** ("60 FPS") and **frame time** ("17 ms, slowest 17 ms"); top right CPU, GPU, RAM, VRAM, CPU temperature, GPU temperature and Power, each "CPU: 24 %", "RAM: 3.2 GB of 8 GB", "Power: 18.4 W". No blur, no shadow, no animation, and a line is redrawn only when its text changes. It is disabled (`enabled: false`), takes no focus, is not in the focus chain and eats no key: the game keeps every press. **Every state says what it is, never a bare dash:** a measured number; *unsupported* (the console has no source) "<label>: Not available on this console" (every value is in `text`: the words carry the state, 04 §7); *unavailable* (a rate's first reading, less than a second of frames counted, a failed request) "<label>: Measuring..."; *stale* (the reading failed just now, the last good value is under 10 s old) the number followed by "(last reading)". The frame lines with no app surface (`Performance.frameSource` null) say "FPS: No game in front"; RAM and VRAM show used of total and take the worse state of the two. Nothing is estimated (ADR-033 point 4). **Held numbers are never shown as live:** a failed `GetPerformance` and the first show after idle (the overlay was hidden, the answer in `Performance.metrics` is from before) mark every held entry `unavailable` with value 0 until a fresh answer arrives (`Performance.fresh` is false meanwhile); an `unsupported` entry stays. Sampling runs only while the overlay is shown (`PerformanceConsumer { active: shown }` in `PerformanceReadout`: off, Home, the Hub, a splash and the saver each stop it); the interval is the choice row (1 s default). A profile that is deleted loses its switch (`Shell.qml` clears it on `Profiles.deleteFinished` "ok"), so a reused id does not inherit it. Cost on the Dell (FPS of a game with the overlay on and off) is the lead's run (T-469, hw pending). **Where the numbers come from (T-451, built):** the `Performance` singleton (`shell/src/performance`). FPS and frame time are the compositor's (`FrameMeter`): the front app surface's commits (`redraw()`, one per `wl_surface.commit`; `Compositor.qml` sets `Performance.frameSource` to the surface of the app in front, `Shell.focusedApp` while an app is open, and `frameWindow` to the shell's window) and the shell's presented frames (`frameSwapped`); a presented frame counts when the app committed since the frame before, so a game committing 120 times a second on a 60 Hz display shows 60. Over the last second: `fps`, `frameTimeMs` (mean interval) and `frameTimeP99Ms` (99th percentile, nearest rank), `commitsPerSecond`; `frameState` is `supported` after a whole second of counting, `unavailable` before that and with no app in front ("Measuring..."), `unsupported` without a compositor window (the Windows host). The rest is `Devices1.System.GetPerformance` (contract in the XML, `03`), as `metrics` (`stateOf(name)`, `valueOf(name)`). Nothing runs while nobody looks: a `PerformanceConsumer { active: ... }` (the overlay holds one) starts the polling (at once, then every `DeviceSettings.performanceIntervalMs`, 500-5000 ms, default 1000, Settings key `hub/performanceIntervalMs`) and connects the frame meter; with no consumer the service is not asked and no commit is counted.
- **Notifications** (T-245): the history, newest first, and Clear; quiet mode is the tile.
- **Power** (built): the power choices of §1.4 (Suspend, Restart, Power off as logind allows), then **Switch profile** (T-471: moved here from the Quick Hub; it closes the Hub, then locks: the picker) and Back; the list has the title "Power". **The game pauses (owner, 2026-11-10; T-457).** Opening the Hub over an app freezes it (its picture stays under the Hub, its sound stops); Resume, B or Guide thaws it and gives it the focus back in the same frame the Hub starts to slide out. Home leaves it frozen behind Home (ADR-019); Close game thaws it first, then stops it (and shows the closing screen, §1.5); Switch to thaws the chosen app and leaves the old one frozen. The game card says "Paused · 12 min" while frozen (T-471). Settings -> System has "Pause games when Ruya Hub opens" (on by default; off for online games). When freezing fails (the service refuses or times out) the Hub opens anyway and the game keeps running; nothing is shown as paused. **As built (T-457, `HubPause.qml`, host-tested `tst_hub_pause.qml`; hw pending):** the Hub opens at once and asks `Session1.Freeze` for the app in front (the Session1 instance of the focused app); "Paused" (the game card's state line, `hubCardState`: "Paused · 12 min", body size, demi-bold; T-471) shows only once the service reports the record `frozen`. The game card is as tall as the taller of its art and its text, so a two-line title over "Paused · 12 min" never runs into Home | Close game. The shell remembers which apps it asked to freeze: whenever the Hub is closed and one of them is in front again it calls `Thaw`, so Resume, B and Guide thaw the app they return to, Home leaves it frozen until its Running card (or anything else) brings it to the front, "Switch to" thaws the chosen app only if it was frozen, and a power choice that closes the Hub over the app thaws it like Resume. Switch profile closes the Hub before it locks, so the Thaw normally goes out while the profile is still signed in and the app runs on behind the picker as before (freezing a locked profile's apps is T-165's); an app left frozen behind Home when the profile switches stays frozen until its profile is back and it is in front. **The sweep:** a frozen app of the signed-in profile that the shell did not freeze (a shell restart while a game was frozen, a Thaw refused because the lock landed first, a Freeze served after its Thaw) is thawed on every change of the running list and at every sign-in. A Thaw that fails toasts "Could not resume the game. Open Ruya Hub and choose Resume." (`warning`); the sweep or the next Hub over the game (its Freeze is a no-op for the service, its close thaws) tries again. Close game calls `Stop` only: the service thaws a frozen app before it stops it (`tst_hub_pause` sees the mock's thaw, then the stop). A refused or failed Freeze is forgotten, so no Thaw follows. The setting is `DeviceSettings.pauseGamesInHub` (`hub/pauseGames`, on by default), a toggle row in Settings -> System for every profile, with the note "Online games can lose their connection while paused."; off means no Freeze and no Thaw. **A frozen game stays frozen until the player resumes it (T-464):** when the window in front goes away (a game that closes its first window and opens a second, or crashes at start), `Shell.fallbackFront()` never makes a frozen app (one in `HubPause.asked`, or the service reports `frozen`) the front app by itself; if only frozen apps are left, Home shows (the apps are behind Home, their Running cards bring them back) or the launch splash while a launch is on. Belt and braces in `HubPause.rebelt()`: a game the Hub froze that runs again while another app or Home is in front is frozen again; a game that is closing (`state` not `running`) is left alone. It is in HubPause because `asked` is its state and its own Thaws leave `asked` first, so Resume, Switch to and B are never undone.

**Focus and Back.** Guide toggles the Hub from anywhere, also from inside a panel (it closes the whole Hub and resumes). B in a panel returns to the Quick Hub with the focus on that panel's icon; B in the Quick Hub closes it (Resume over an app). Every open starts on the game card (over an app) or on the first row there is: the last panel and the last column are not remembered, so Guide then A always resumes the game, which is what a player in the middle of a game expects. The hint bar shows `A` Resume on the game card, `B` Back in a panel and `B` Resume in the Quick Hub. No key reaches the app while the Hub is open (built).

**Budgets** (T-455; `scripts/dell-hub-check.sh` measures them on the Dell, PASS/FAIL, `docs/dev-setup.md`). The shell writes `page-open hub create= firstSwap= afterAnim=` to the journal for every Hub open that settles (the same probe as a page's, T-349; ms from the Guide key reaching the shell). Budgets, with the first Dell numbers (image 0.0.66 plus the T-455 shell, UHD 630, 1080p, SuperTuxKart at 60 FPS):
- **Open:** the median first frame after Guide under 100 ms (measured 23 ms, worst 47 ms in two runs of ten; the slide ends at 215 ms).
- **Hidden:** the Hub draws no frame of its own; what a game costs the shell is its compositing, at most 20 % GPU and 20 % of a core at 60 FPS (measured 11 % and 11 %). A guard rail, not a proof of "no frame".
- **Open over a frozen game** (the default): under 3 % GPU, the picture is still (measured 5 ms render and 0 ticks of CPU in 10 s).
- **Game FPS** with the Hub open and the pause setting off: a drop under 10 % (measured 60 against 60). With the pause on (the default) the game is frozen by design and the FPS is 0.
The compositor's frame meter logs `perf-frames` four times a second when the shell runs with `RUYA_PERF_LOG=1` (a measuring switch for that script, not a setting).

### 1.2 Settings page (as built, T-103)
- Two panes. Left: the category list (My profile, Manage profiles for an admin only, Network, Bluetooth & controllers, Display & motion, Audio, Storage, Accessibility, System, About). Right: the selected category's pane. Its modals (PIN pad, keyboard, confirmations, the Resolution / Refresh rate / Wallpaper / Time zone pickers, the pairing screen) are built the first time they open and then kept; a pane's service refreshes wait until it is first shown (the sound themes one frame later). The Network singleton is created when the shell starts, so its list is there when the pane is first shown.

A row keeps the keys it uses: a ChoiceRow or SliderRow consumes Left/Right, so those never leave the pane (leave with Back); ToggleRow and SettingRow leave Left to the pane, which returns to the list.

The **game page** (`ActivityGamePage`, `Shell.openPage("activityGame", { appId })`): time played, times played, first and last played, longest and average session, and a 30-day strip. The week is the last seven days ending today (a rolling week, not Monday to Sunday). Hint bar: `B` Back on the chart and the read-only rows, `A` Select elsewhere. The shell tells the service who is in front (`UsagePresence.qml`: the newest running app of the active profile while an app item has the screen and the Guide overlay is closed; "idle" after 10 minutes without input; "locked" with nobody signed in; on every change and every 20 s).

(`warning`) and the row shows what the service has; the pane asks for the level again (`GetBrightness`) whenever it is shown, as a laptop's own keys change it unseen), **UI size** (a `ChoiceRow`: Auto, 100, 125, 150, 200 %; applies at once, `04` §2) and **Reset display settings** (Accept puts the preferred mode and Auto back; toast "Display settings reset."; the mode part restarts the shell like a change, but needs no confirmation). A mode change (and a reset that changes the mode) is **refused while a game or app runs** (the restart would kill it: toast "Close running games and apps first...") and while the service says the compositor has restarted too often in the last two minutes (toast "Try again in a minute..."; `03` §2.4). B or Back, Revert, or the countdown reaching zero restore the previous mode and restart the shell again, **whatever the service answers** (a failed Revert restarts too: the compositor's guard at the next start drops a marker that is still there, or rewrites the config from the stored choice); nobody has to see the screen for the revert to happen. A Keep the service refuses shows an error toast and the countdown goes on, so it still reverts. The dialog is modal (every key is taken; Left/Right move between the buttons), its card and texts are capped to the screen width so they wrap at 200 %, and the hint bar's A names the focused button (A Revert / A Keep, B Revert). The shell opens Settings -> Display & motion under it when it comes back, so a Keep returns focus there. If the shell never got to ask (it crashed, or the console was powered off on a black screen), the compositor's next start drops the choice (`03` §2.4) and the shell says once "The screen did not answer, so the previous resolution is back." (warning toast; asked again when the service appears, not only at start). A refused change ("The screen mode could not be changed.", error toast) shows no dialog. Only the first connected output is used.

### 1.4a Power saving (T-202; the timeline and the inhibitors are in `01` §2.2)
- **One idle clock** (`IdleClock.qml`, a singleton): `KeyRouter.activity` (any key, controller button, the Guide button, pointer or touch event the window receives) calls `IdleClock.noteInput()`; the clock reads **a monotonic source** (`MonotonicClock`, a `QElapsedTimer`; never the wall clock, so NTP, a time zone or a date set by hand changes no stage, T-293); `idleSeconds` is the time since, `quietSeconds` the same with held-awake time counted as activity, and `stage` is `active`, `saver`, `off` or `sleep`. The update report (`Update.noteActivity`), `LiquidGate`, the toasts and `UsagePresence` hear input from its `inputNoted` signal; none keeps a clock of its own. The 60-second throttle of `Update.noteActivity` only limits bus calls.
- **Screen saver** (`ScreenSaver.qml`, z 100 above everything, the OSD and dialogs included): a near-black ground (`Theme.saverGround`, the same in every theme, so it is dark on Daylight too), the installed apps' art (hero, else tile, else the generated art) at `Theme.saverArtOpacity` drifting and cross-fading every `Theme.saverSlideMs`, and a clock (`hh:mm`) that takes a new place every minute inside the safe area (burn-in safe). No focus ring and nothing takes focus. **Input**: `KeyRouter.swallowInput` is on while any stage but `active` is up: the first key, button, pointer or touch event is reported to the clock and then eaten (with its release, and the repeats of a held key), so it wakes the console and reaches no page or app; the page keeps the focus it had. A key that was eaten and not seen released is forgotten when the window is deactivated or after 2 s without a repeat, so a lost release cannot eat a later press. **Performance tier**: no art, only the dark ground and the moving clock. **Reduced motion**: one still dimmed frame (no drift, no cross-fade); the clock still jumps to its new place. **PIN rule (decision)**: the saver starts even over a PIN pad, but every open `PinPad` is cancelled when it starts (`IdleClock.saverStarted`), so nothing typed is left and no field is active behind it; the picker is back on its list after waking.
- **Behind an app (T-376)**: while an app is in front (`appOpen`, also under the Guide overlay) the whole backdrop layer L0 (backdrop, wallpaper layer, hero art) is not drawn (opacity 0, T-387: not `visible: false`), so nothing is drawn behind the opaque app and no frame is spent on it; the status cluster's glass, which blurs L0, is hidden with it (the nav already is), so no effect samples a hidden layer. The ambient bed stops its players (`02` 5.3). Home's fade covers L0 coming back.
- **Display off**: stage `off`: a black frame, the clock hidden, no timer or animation running, the backdrop paused (`Backdrop.shown`; also paused, `Backdrop.paused`, while an app is in front and covers Home, T-293); `ScreenPower.setOn(false)` switches the display off through the platform plugin (`QPlatformScreen::setPowerState`, DPMS under `eglfs_kms`; on other platforms only the black frame). The first input switches it on and wakes, and **input in the second after the screen came back is eaten too** (`IdleClock.wakeGapMs` = 1000, a constant in `IdleClock`, to be tuned on the Dell; `IdleClock.swallowInput` = `covered || wakeGuard` feeds `KeyRouter.swallowInput`): a press while the TV is still re-syncing only counts as activity. It applies after the dark screen and after sleep, not after the saver. How a TV re-syncs is for the Dell to record (T-202 Outcome).
- **Sleep**: stage `sleep`: `Power.suspend()`. Not entered unless `Power.sleepAllowed`. **A resume restarts the clock from `active`** in every stage: the monotonic clock stops while the machine sleeps, so the resume is read from `MonotonicClock.sleptMs` (CLOCK_BOOTTIME minus CLOCK_MONOTONIC on Linux; 0 elsewhere), which jumps across a suspend; a clock that does count the sleep shows as a gap above `gapMs` and is read the same way.
- **Idle controllers**: every minute while the console has been idle for the controller time, and at once when input ends an idle period, the shell sends `Bluetooth.reportIdle(idle, timeout, hold)` (`Devices1.Bluetooth.ReportIdle`). `hold` is true while an app is in front (the shell does not see every button a game uses: X, Y, the triggers and the right stick are not mapped by `ruya-inputd`) or something holds the console awake (an install or update included, so a pad is never switched off during an install or update screen). The service (`idle_pads.rs`) disconnects each connected, paired controller once per idle period; a pad that reconnects gets the whole time again. The idle time is the console's, not the pad's: with two pads, input on one keeps both on.
- **Settings -> Power saving** (an own category before System; `PowerSavingPane`): Screen saver (Off, 5, 10, 15, 30 minutes), Turn the screen off (Never, 15, 30 minutes, 1 hour), Sleep (Never, 30 minutes, 1, 2, 4 hours; where logind allows suspend but `/usr/share/ruyaos/suspend-verified` does not exist yet the row stays, reads "Sleep is not available on this console yet", is disabled (takes no focus, no value) and the step does not exist; without `CanSuspend` the row is hidden), Controllers turn off (Never, 5, 10, 15, 30 minutes) and a read-only "Keeping the console awake" row that names what holds the console awake ("a download or install", "a system update", "audio playing", "an app"; the log has the exact reasons). Each row writes `DeviceSettings` at once and the clock follows without a restart. When a later step is set at or before an earlier one (for example the screen off at 15 minutes with the saver at 30) one extra row says so ("The screen saver never shows: The screen turns off first"; also for sleep: "The screen is not turned off: The console sleeps instead"); the choices stay free. A Power mode row (Balanced, Power saver, Performance) waits for `power-profiles-daemon` in the image. The saver's clock is `TimeZone.formatClock` (the console's zone, like the status bar), not the process zone. The look is in `04` §5 (Screen saver) and its tokens in §3.1 and §3.4.

## 2. Visual language
Defined in [`04-design-system.md`](04-design-system.md) ("Dreamglass", ADR-012): principles, colour/type/spacing/radius/motion tokens, focus, depth, the component catalogue, copy rules and the screenshot review list. In short: dark theme, 10-foot type (body 30 px, min 24 px at 1080p), 5 % safe area, frosted glass over blurred hero art, the focused item scales to 1.08 with a white ring that is never hidden, and every value is a `Theme` token.

## 3. Motion & effects

### 3.1 Techniques
| Technique | Use | Caveats |
|---|---|---|
| `Behavior on x/opacity/scale` | Implicit motion for focus, cards | Don't combine with an explicit animation on the same property |
| `states` + `transitions` | Page and panel layouts | Keep the state count small and named |
| `Sequential/ParallelAnimation` | Choreographed sequences (launch, boot-in) | Must be interruptible: a new input cancels and settles |
| Animate `opacity`, `scale`, `x/y`, `rotation` only | GPU-cheap | Avoid animating `width/height`, layout or text |
| `layer.enabled` / `ShaderEffectSource` | Cache static subtrees and the **single shared blurred backdrop** | Costs VRAM; invalidate rarely |
| `MultiEffect` (blur, shadow, colourise) | Frosted panels, glow | Keep `blurMax` low on low tier; blur a downscaled source |
| Custom `ShaderEffect` | Special effects | Shaders precompiled to `.qsb` via `qt_add_shaders()` in CMake; **no inline GLSL in Qt 6** |

Blur pattern: render the backdrop (hero art) once to a quarter-resolution `ShaderEffectSource`, apply `MultiEffect` blur to that, and share the result among all panels. Re-blur only when the hero art changes, with a crossfade.

### 3.2 Durations
Focus move 120–160 ms, page transition 250–350 ms, overlay open 200 ms, all ease-out. These are defined as tokens. **Reduced motion** sets the `focusMs`, `pageMs` and `tintMs` tokens to 0, removes the page slide (`pageSlide` = 0) and stops the backdrop drift. (Implemented as 0, not 60 ms crossfades.) Every duration token is its base value times `motionScale`, which is 0 under reduced motion (T-204). The card glare and the art-placeholder shimmer (04 §3.8) do not run either, while shadows stay. The feedback animations are also off under reduced motion: the PIN-pad wrong-PIN shake and the Accept pulse on art cards do not run at all (the error message and sound, or the launch toast, remain). Their sizes and durations are `Theme` tokens (`shakeDistance`, `shakeQuickMs`, `shakeSlowMs`, `pulseScale`, `pulseDownMs`, `pulseUpMs`). The setting is per device (`DeviceSettings`), and `--reduced-motion` forces it for a session.

### 3.3 Quality tiers
`Effects = High | Balanced | Performance`, chosen automatically at first boot from the GPU (renderer string plus a quick benchmark) and overridable in Settings. **Implemented so far:** the per-device `effectsQuality` setting and the `--effects` flag; automatic detection is still open (needs real hardware).

**Heat (T-200):** when the console runs hot the shell drops to Performance for the session (`DeviceSettings.thermalLimited`: `effectsQuality` reads `performance`, `storedEffectsQuality` keeps the owner's choice) until the temperatures are normal again (`01` §2.4).

Token values per tier (`Theme.qml`): `blurMax` is 64 / 32 / 0, and the glass `MultiEffect`s (`GlassPill`) are hidden at 0, where the pills, the glass sheets and the glass that lies straight on L0 on a page (the Settings rows, T-434) use the more opaque `glassOpaque` token. Ambient drift of the backdrop runs on High and Balanced, and stops on Performance. ## 4. Input & focus model

### 4.1 Event path
Physical pad → input daemon (ADR-003) → shell. While the shell has input focus, the daemon emits navigation as key events (Up/Down/Left/Right, Accept, Back, Menu, Guide, LB/RB, LT/RT) to the compositor's seat. While an app is focused, only **Guide** (and the volume keys) reach the shell. As built (T-150): the C++ `KeyRouter` (event filter on the window, `shell/src/input/`) takes the Guide keys, including Super and the Guide hold, from a focused app item and hands them to the shell; the volume keys are application shortcuts matched before any key event; every other key reaches the app by normal focus delivery (the router counts focus on the app item or any item inside it). In the image (T-130) the app items are `ShellSurfaceItem`s, one per xdg toplevel, which forward the keys they get to their Wayland client. `KeyRouter.isGuideKey` is the one definition of which keys are Guide. Keyboard and mouse go through libinput and the compositor as normal.

### 4.2 Mapping
| Action | Xbox / generic | PlayStation | Nintendo | Keyboard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Accept | A | ✕ (○ with "Confirm button: right", the JP layout) | A (right) | Enter |
| Back | B | ○ | B (bottom) | Esc / Backspace |
| Context menu | Menu (☰) | Options | + | `M` / context key |
| Guide / overlay | Xbox | PS | Home | `Super` (left or right; holding it opens the power choices) |
| Tab switch | LB/RB | L1/R1 | L/R | `Q`/`E`, PgUp/PgDn |
| Fast scroll | LT/RT, stick held | L2/R2 | ZL/ZR | Home/End |

**Target (ADR-027, T-398; the full table, the action names and the Settings > Controllers "Confirm button" row are [06 §11](06-input.md)).** The family decides Accept and Back, per event in the input service, never in QML. **As built today** `ruya-inputd` maps by position for every family (south = Accept, east = Back), so a Nintendo pad's A is Back until T-398.

As built (T-179): the singleton `BackKey` (`shell/qml/BackKey.qml`) is the one definition of which keys are Back. `BackKey.isBackOrBackspace(key)` (Back, Esc, Backspace) is what pages, Settings panes, the section nav, the setup wizard, the Guide overlay and the confirm dialogs use. The text fields (`TextEntry`, `PinPad`) use `BackKey.isBack(key)` (Back, Esc) only, because there Backspace deletes a character and never goes back. Back acts on every key event, as Esc always did; it does not filter auto-repeat.

**Tab (T-285, T-282 item 8).** Tab is unmapped on the row lists that are not Settings panes (Power, the setup wizard, the filters, the app detail page): it does nothing there, as there is no Tab button on a pad. In a Settings pane Tab moves down a row, scrolling like Down (T-190).

### 4.3 Rules
- Every screen has exactly one `FocusScope` root, and every interactive item is reachable with the D-pad. Directional navigation uses `KeyNavigation` or a small spatial-navigation helper (nearest item in the direction pressed).
- Focus is never lost. When the focused item disappears, focus moves to the nearest sibling, else to the scope's default.
- Focus is restored per surface and per profile (returning to Home focuses the last-launched tile).
- Key repeat on hold: 400 ms delay, then accelerating repeat. The analog stick maps to the same, with speed proportional to deflection.
- Long-press (≥ 500 ms) on Accept opens the context menu, with a visible progress ring.
- Modal dialogs trap focus. The PIN pad accepts only digits, Back and Accept. While its profile is locked out the digits, Del and OK are dimmed and ignored, and the only message is the countdown; the pad works again when it reaches 0.
- On the profile picker, any controller may act. After login, the logged-in profile's controller is "player 1". **Target (ADR-027, T-397):** after sign-in only the active profile's **owner pads** and the keyboard navigate; another pad's press does nothing but a one-time toast, its Guide offers "Switch to <profile>?" or "Who's playing?", and when every owner pad is gone the next pad's press offers the takeover (Accept; the PIN first on a PIN-protected profile; never by an idle timeout). The states table is [06 §11.3](06-input.md). As built, every pad navigates.
- **Hint bar (T-158).** One `HintBar` in the shell's top layer says what the buttons do on the screen on top. The screen supplies the hints: a page through `Page.hints` (default `A` Select, `B` Back; a page may override it), Home `A` Select and one `LB RB` Sections hint (two glyphs, one verb), the setup wizard its own hints (`Setup.hints`: `A` Next, or Select on the profile step, `B` Back except on the first and last step; `B` Cancel over its PIN pad (its keyboard supplies its own hint, below); `B` Skip, with `A` Try again after a timeout, on the controller pairing stage), a page with nothing to select (placeholder pages and categories without a pane) only `B` Back (`Page.backOnlyHints`); Settings > About and System information show `B` Back only while a read-only row has focus and `A` Select on the rows that act (the System information row, Save report, Include network addresses), the quick menu `A` Select and `B` Resume over an app, `B` Close without one (`B` Back in the power choices), the launch splash nothing (`A` Close, `B` Close once it offers Close), a modal whose focus is in the modal layer or the picker's PIN pad `B` Cancel (the on-screen keyboard supplies its own hints as `TextEntry.entryHints`, T-194, T-285: `A` Type, `B` Delete while a controller's Back would delete a letter and `B` Cancel when the field is empty, `Start` Done; after typing on a physical keyboard `A` goes, since Enter finishes), the picker `A` Select. A modal in the modal layer that takes Accept declares `modalHints` (`A` Select, `B` Cancel): the wallpaper and mode pickers, the zone chooser, `PowerConfirm` and the uninstall confirmation; the Power saving pane (all `ChoiceRow`s) says `Left/Right` Change, and the Activity charts `Left/Right` Browse while a chart row has focus (the glyph is a pill with that word, as the bar has no D-pad glyph). It is hidden while an app is focused (a Guide overlay over an app still shows its own), never takes focus, and on Home sits under the Recent row.
- The pointer is shown only after mouse movement and hidden after 3 s idle.

### 4.4 Text entry (as built, T-104)
`TextEntry.qml` is the modal on-screen keyboard; every flow that needs text uses it. `open(title, initialText, { maxLength, mask, layout })`, signals `accepted(text)` and `cancelled()`. `layout` is `text`, `email` (adds `@`, `.`, `.com`) or `url` (adds `/`, `.`, `.com`); `mask` shows dots but `accepted` carries the real text; `maxLength` 0 means unlimited. The layout is data (`layoutFor`): rows of keys with a width in key units, on a letters page and a symbols page (the `#+=`/`ABC` key switches).

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| D-pad / stick | Move over the keys. Left/Right wrap within a row; Up/Down go to the key whose centre is nearest in x (ties go left; no wrap vertically) |
| Accept (A, `Return`) | Press the focused key |
| Back (B, `Escape`) | Delete one character; cancel when the field is empty (`Backspace` never cancels, see below) |
| Start (`Menu`) / keypad `Enter` | Done (does nothing while the field is empty) |
| LB / RB (`PageUp` / `PageDown`) | Jump to Del / Space |
| Shift key | One-shot capital for the next character |
| Physical keyboard | Printable characters insert; `Backspace` deletes |

A controller's A and B arrive as `Return` and `Escape`, the same keys a keyboard sends. So once the user types on a physical keyboard (a printable key or `Backspace`), `Return` means Done and `Escape` means Cancel, until the next D-pad key switches back to controller behaviour. The field is append/delete at the end only. The component swallows every key (including Guide and volume keys' key events) and returns focus to the item that had it when it opened. Not yet used by any flow (T-105 is the first).

## 5. Audio (UI side)
- Channels: **UI SFX** (the cues of §5.3), **Ambient** (the loop of §5.3; on by default at the slider's default level, owner to confirm after listening), **Media/App** (everything else).
- These are implemented as PipeWire stream roles (`media.role = Notification|Music|Game|Movie`). WirePlumber policy does **ducking**: Ambient is lowered by about 8 dB while a UI cue or app audio plays, and Ambient is muted whenever an app is in the foreground.
- SFX playback uses `QSoundEffect` with preloaded WAVs. Configure a low PipeWire quantum for the SFX stream to hit the latency KPI.
- Separate volume sliders for UI, Ambient and Master. The volume OSD appears on any volume change, including controller or keyboard media keys.
- Mild stereo panning of navigation SFX by focus x-position (on in High, off otherwise).
- Output switching (HDMI / analog / USB / Bluetooth) follows WirePlumber defaults with a "prefer most recently connected" rule. The shell shows a toast. Covered in 03 §2.3.

### 5.1 Toasts and volume OSD (as built, T-108)
- `Notifications.show(text, kind, iconText)` (QML singleton; kind `info|success|warning|error`, unknown kinds become `info`) queues a toast. `ToastLayer` shows at most 3 at the top right, below the header and inside the safe area; the rest wait in order. Each toast dismisses itself after `Theme.toastMs` (4000 ms), fading and sliding with `Theme.pageMs`/`pageSlide` (so reduced motion removes the movement).
- **Sticky toasts (T-159):** `Notifications.showSticky(key, text, kind)` shows a toast that does not dismiss itself (no timer) and stays until `Notifications.remove(key)`; one per key (showing the key again replaces it), and it can be removed while it still waits in the queue. The first user is the restart notice: while `Update.restartPending` (the service's `restart_at` is set, `01` §4) a `warning` toast "Restarting to finish the update in 2 minutes. Press any button to wait." is up, and it goes when the record clears it. `Update.noteActivity` reports every input to the service while a restart is pending (instead of once a minute), because that press is the one that calls the restart off.
- `Audio` (C++ singleton over `org.ruyaos.Devices1.Audio`, see §5.2) holds the master `volume` (0-100) and `muted`, with `setVolume`, `stepVolume(delta)`, `toggleMute()` and `setMuted()`. `adjusted()` fires after every master command, even a clamped one (and when the master level or mute changes from outside), but not for a channel volume alone.
- **Volume keys while muted** (T-114): Volume Up unmutes and raises; Volume Down stays muted and changes nothing (the OSD shows "Muted"); Mute toggles only the mute.
- `VolumeOsd` (bottom centre) appears on `Audio.adjusted` and hides `Theme.osdMs` (1500 ms) after the last one. It shows the level and a "Muted" state.
- Volume Up/Down/Mute are `Shortcut`s (application context) at the `Shell` root, so they work in every state, modals included, step by 5, and never move focus. Toasts and the OSD are display-only and never take focus.
- Stacking: toasts (z 10) sit above pages and below the modals (profile picker, quick menu: z 20; the launch splash is z 6, above the app layer and below the toasts); the OSD is on top (z 30).

## 6. Asset pipeline
- Per app, the art set (from AppStream/`x-ruya-*` keys, or our fallback generator) is icon (SVG or 256 px), tile (16:9), hero (16:9, ≥ 1920 px), logo (transparent), and an optional background.
- On install, `ruya-store` produces resized variants (720p/1080p/2160p) in WebP into `/var/cache/ruyaos/art/`. The shell loads only the variant matching the quality tier and display, via a `QQuickImageProvider` (`image://art/<appid>/<kind>`).
- `Image { asynchronous: true; cache: true; sourceSize: … }` always. Show a placeholder or blurhash while loading.
- **Budget:** ≤ 100 MB of textures on the low tier and ≤ 300 MB on mid/high. Off-screen rows unload their art. One shared blurred backdrop.
- Shell-owned assets (sounds, icons, fonts) are compiled into the binary as Qt resources.

## 7. Accessibility & internationalisation
- Text scale (100–200%), high-contrast theme, reduced motion, mono audio, focus-ring size.
- Screen reader: expose `Accessible.*` properties everywhere. Orca support on a custom compositor is **unknown**, so spike it in Phase 2 and record the result.
- Subtitles are app-owned. The shell only offers a system-wide preference that it passes to apps where a standard exists.
- All strings use `qsTr()`. Translations are Qt `.ts` files managed in a translation platform. Layouts use anchors/`LayoutMirroring` so RTL works (tested with a pseudo-RTL locale). Initial locales: en, tr (others later).
- **Built (T-109).** Translations are `shell/i18n/ruyashell_<lang>.ts` (`qt_add_translations`, embedded as `:/i18n/ruyashell_<lang>.qm`; English is the source language and its file carries the plural forms). `DeviceSettings.language` (`en`/`tr`) installs the `QTranslator` and calls `QQmlEngine::retranslate()`, so changing it in Settings -> System (or the first-boot language step) re-translates the open UI at once. Default: the stored value, else the system locale, else `en`. Messages stored in a property when an event happened (PIN-pad and Settings status lines, toasts) keep their language until the next event. Lists that are models never change with the language (they hold keys; the labels are bindings), because a changed model would reset `currentIndex` and lose focus. The on-screen keyboard follows the language: Turkish adds ç ğ ı ö ş ü and the Q layout's extra keys (narrower keys, rows stay 10 units); Shift maps i to İ and ı to I. `ruya-shell --reverse` lays the UI out right-to-left (`LayoutMirroring`), a readiness check: sign-in, the home tiles and Settings stay navigable (tested); the remaining RTL gaps are listed in the T-109 Outcome. A completeness lint (`scripts/lint-translations.ps1`) fails on unfinished entries.

## 9. Performance rules
1. Never block the GUI thread. All I/O goes over async D-Bus calls. `WorkerScript` is for pure CPU work only.
2. Bindings and JavaScript should take ≤ 2 ms per frame. Profile with the QML Profiler, GammaRay and `perf` after each feature.
3. Hide off-screen items (`visible: false`). Don't put alpha on fullscreen layers except the one backdrop.
4. Keep V-Sync on. Never set a custom render loop frame rate.
5. Test every effect change on the low tier before merging (CI KPI job on the reference box).

### 5.3 Sound themes, the cue set and the ambient bed (T-227; the maker's guide is `docs/developers/sound-themes.md`)
- **A sound theme** is a folder with `theme.json` (`name`, `author`, `licence` as an SPDX id, `version`, `cues`: cue name to file, optional `ambient`: `file`, `loop` {`start`, `end`} in ms, `gain` 0-1), the audio files and a README. Cues: WAV PCM 16-bit, mono or stereo, 48 kHz, at most 1.5 s and 200 KB, peak below -3 dBFS. Ambient: Ogg Vorbis, Ogg Opus or FLAC, at most 3 MB, a loop of at least 5 s inside the file. `theme.json` at most 64 KB, at most 64 cues. A path in the manifest is plain and relative (no `..`, no drive letter, no link). **The checker** (`src/sound/SoundThemeCheck.cpp`, a pure function from a folder to ok/problems/warnings) enforces all of this reading the folder as data only: sizes before any read, WAV parsed in memory, the ambient file read at its head and tail for its length (FLAC STREAMINFO, the Ogg identification header and the last page's granule), and for FLAC the header of the first audio frame after the metadata blocks, which must name its sample rate (rate code 0, "as in STREAMINFO", is refused: Qt 6.11's FFmpeg player opens its output at 0 Hz for it, the bed never sounds and Qt retries several times a second, T-321), nothing decoded by a codec library, nothing executed. **The ambient must be heard on a TV and a 5 cm speaker (T-330):** a FLAC ambient (read whole, at most 3 MB) is decoded by the checker's own decoder (RFC 9639: every subframe type, every stereo layout; the CRC-8 and CRC-16 of every frame must match, every count and shift is checked before use, a sample must fit its bit depth; at most 10 minutes of audio are decoded whatever STREAMINFO claims) over the first minute of the loop, and refused when more than half of its energy is below 150 Hz (a 4th-order Butterworth low-pass at 150 Hz) or when the median RMS of its 1 s windows is below -40 dBFS (before the gain), and when a frame is damaged or the audio ends before the loop does. An Ogg ambient is not decoded; it passes with a warning that its level and bass were not checked. The bundled beds (`scripts/make-sounds.py`) have their energy between 200 Hz and 4 kHz at -22.5 LUFS, true peak below -3 dBFS, and the generator's `--verify` fails outside -26 to -22 LUFS, above 3 % below 120 Hz or under 85 % between 200 Hz and 4 kHz. The shell, `ruya-shell --check-sound-theme <folder>` and `scripts/lint-sound-themes.ps1` use this one function. The ambient formats are Ogg and FLAC because the bundled theme is generated by a script that has a FLAC encoder but no Vorbis one.
- **Cue set** (the names are the contract): `navigate` (focus moves), `select`, `back`, `error` (wrong PIN, a refused action), `notification` (a toast), `warning` (a warning or error toast, a sticky toast), `open`/`close` (the Guide overlay), `page` (a section changes), `volume` (the volume bar), `startup` (once, at the first frame the shell draws), `toggle-on`/`toggle-off` (a switch). `Sfx.qml` has one function per cue (`toggled(checked)` picks the toggle cue) and a counter for each; `playedCount` counts the cues that really sounded. A cue the active theme does not name plays from the bundled theme; a cue no theme has is silent. The UI sounds channel scales every cue (navigate keeps its 0.6 base, volume 0.8); reduced motion does not silence sound, UI sounds at 0 does (the request is counted, nothing plays and the ambient is not ducked). **No cue plays while the screen saver, the dark screen or sleep is up** (`IdleClock.covered`). Where a toast, a wrong PIN or a refusal has its own cue, the older `back` sound was taken out so one event makes one sound.
- **Where themes live**: the bundled theme "Dreamglass" is under `/usr/share/ruyaos/sounds/dreamglass/` (installed by `shell/CMakeLists.txt`). The Windows dev build and the Linux test build also embed the theme as a Qt resource (CMake option `RUYA_SOUNDS_RESOURCE`, ON there); the image build (`RUYA_COMPOSITOR=ON`) leaves it out, so `ruya-shell` does not carry a second copy of 2.5 MB (1.1 MB before the T-330 bed), and a missing image folder then means the bundled theme is unusable (a log line, no cues). A USB drive's themes are `RuyaOS/sounds/<folder>/` (folder names of letters, digits, `.`, `_`, `-`; at most 16 looked at per drive; the checker runs in a worker thread, `SoundThemes.refresh`). Choosing one **copies** it (all regular files, no links, at most 100 files and 12 MB, exactly that much is fine; a theme over a limit is refused as "too-many-files" or "too-large" with its own toast; re-checked after copying) to the shell's data directory (`<AppData>/sounds/<folder>`, `RUYA_SOUND_DIR` overrides it); at most 8 copies; nothing is ever loaded from the drive in place. A chosen theme that fails the checker is ignored with a log line and a warning toast, and the bundled theme plays (`SoundThemes.selectedBroken`). The choice is `DeviceSettings.soundTheme` (`dreamglass` or `user:<folder>`, per device).
- **Ambient bed** (`AmbientLoop.qml`, in `Shell`): plays on Home and the menus (also the profile picker) at the Ambient channel's level times the theme's `gain`; the switch `DeviceSettings.ambientSound` (default on) turns it off. It fades out over 800 ms when an app is in front or the screen saver, the dark screen or sleep starts, and the players are **stopped** when the fade has finished (not paused: a paused player keeps its PipeWire output open and its decoder waking, T-376), so the loop starts again from its loop start on return; it fades in again then. The fade is sound, not motion, so reduced motion does not shorten it. It is lowered by -8 dB (`SoundThemes.duckGain`, ramp `duckRampMs` 120 ms) while a cue sounds: the hold is the cue's own length from the checker (`SoundThemes.cueMs`), at least `duckMs` 450 ms. All audio timing (fade, duck hold and ramp, cross-fade, loop poll) is a constant of `SoundThemes` (`04` 3.9). The loop is two `MediaPlayer`s that take turns: the second starts at the loop start `crossMs` (1.5 s) before the first reaches the loop end and the two cross-fade, so the join never clicks whatever the codec does at a file's end. A theme without `ambient` has no ambient sound (there is no fallback for it). The shell fades and ducks the bed itself; WirePlumber's role ducking/foreground-mute (02 5 first paragraph) is not configured on the image yet and, once it is, may not stack beyond -8 dB in total: decide by listening on the Dell.
- **Settings -> Audio** after the sliders: the **Ambient sound** switch under the Ambient slider, and at the end the **Sound theme** choice (the bundled theme and the copies; Left/Right) followed by one row for every valid theme on a mounted USB drive ("Use "Name" from the USB drive", Accept copies it and switches to it, toast "Sound theme: Name"). The drive rows follow the drives in place, so focus stays; a theme that fails the checker has no theme row but a row "Can't use "Name"" whose value says why in one plain line (`SoundThemeText.qml` turns the checker's first problem into words, en and tr: "A cue is louder than allowed", "The theme.json file has a mistake", ...; never a file name or path); Accept repeats it as a toast, and a copy the checker refuses after copying says the same in its toast. When the chosen theme is a copy, the row **Delete "Name"** sits right under the choice row (never for the bundled theme): Accept opens the confirmation (`PowerConfirm`, Cancel has focus), Confirm resets the setting to Dreamglass and deletes the folder (`SoundThemes.removeTheme`), with no cue of its own, no toast, and focus on the Sound theme row; Back leaves the confirmation and keeps the theme. The toast for "8 copies already" reads "Delete one first, or copy over one with the same name".
- **Dreamglass** is generated by `scripts/make-sounds.py` from numbers (sine partials with a rounded attack for the bells, detuned sine layers with slow level drift for the pad, a 75 s loop in which every frequency and every drift is a whole number of cycles, so the last sample joins the first), CC0, no outside samples. `--verify` (in `dev.ps1 test`) checks determinism, the limits, the seam and that the files in the repository are what the script writes.
