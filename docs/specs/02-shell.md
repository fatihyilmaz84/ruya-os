# RuyaOS — Shell UI/UX

Status: **Draft v1** · Parent: [00-overview](00-overview.md)

The shell (`ruya-shell`) is the only UI the user sees. It's written in Qt 6 Quick/QML with C++ glue. Per ADR-002 (option A, accepted) it is also the Wayland compositor, directly on KMS (T-130; on the Windows host and in the tests it is an ordinary window). It holds no business logic: state and side effects live in the Rust services, which it reaches over D-Bus.

### 1.1 Page model (as built, T-101)
- Home's section nav carries stable keys: `home`, `games`, `media`, `store`, `settings` (`SectionNav.keys`). Accept on a pill (or LB/RB) calls `Shell.openPage(key)` (`home` just focuses Play); a page key `power` exists too, reached from the Guide overlay's own choices and by code; a page opens a detail page on top of itself with `Shell.openPage(key, params)`. `pageStack` holds `{key, params}` entries; `currentPage` is the top key, `currentParams` its params (also the page's `params` property). `closePage()` pops the top entry.
- A page extends `Page.qml`: title below the header, a content area inside the safe area, and Back (`Escape`/`Back`), which plays `Sfx.back()` and closes the page. The page's `focus: true` element gets focus when it opens. Every stack entry has its own live page item inside a slot item under `pageHost`: only the top slot is shown and enabled, lower ones stay alive hidden and disabled, so their scroll position, state and focused item are kept (the shell notes the focused item when a page is pushed over and restores it on pop). A popped slot fades out, then is destroyed once the page on top has finished its fade and slide and that frame is on screen (T-349: a section switch, e.g. Store to Settings, tears the Store page down after the Settings glide, not in its last frame); signing out destroys all of them at once. Pages not in the shell's own list can be registered through `Shell.extraPages` (key -> Component).
- Transition: home fades out and slides left, the page fades in and slides in from the right, over `Theme.pageMs` (`Theme.pageSlide` px); reversed on close. Every page open writes one line to the log (T-349, `PageProbe`): `ruya-shell: page-open <key> create=<ms> firstSwap=<ms> afterAnim=<ms> frames=<n> maxFrame=<ms>`, measured from the key press (`openSection`/`openPage`): the page exists (`create`), the first frame after it is shown (`firstSwap`), the slide and fade have ended (`afterAnim`), the frames shown until then and the longest gap between two of them (`maxFrame`, the worst frame of the transition, the key press counting as the first). A page closed or replaced before it settles writes nothing. A nested page uses the same fade/slide, cross-fading with the page below. The backdrop stays. While a page is open, home is disabled, so arrow keys cannot reach Home.
- Section nav on pages (T-168): one `SectionNav` instance in the shell's top layer (`navShown`: signed in, no app, and either Home or a section page on top; fades with `pageMs`). Its `section` follows the page on screen (`navSection`), so the Settings gear is filled on Settings. `Page.qml` turns an Up that no content item accepted into `upFromTop` (the shell focuses the nav on the page's pill), and PageUp/PageDown into `stepRequested`; Down from the nav restores the page's saved focus; Back from the nav closes the page. Moving between pills moves one gliding highlight under them (T-205, 04 §3.8): a picture only, focus and the nav's accessible name change at once and stay on the nav. Switching section from a page closes the open pages first, so Back lands on Home.
- Closing a page (or the Guide overlay) restores focus to the Home item that had it (a nav pill, Play, Details or the Recent row at its card): `Shell.homeSavedFocus`, set in `openPage` and when the overlay opens, used once by `focusHome()`. `Shell.routeFocus()`/`focusCurrent()` is the single place that decides where focus goes.
- The Guide overlay opens from home or from any page (the shell handles `HomePage` and `Super` when nothing under focus does; `Menu` is the context-menu key, not Guide). Closing it returns focus to the page. "Switch profile" clears the page stack, so the next sign-in lands on home.
- Games, Media and Store are placeholders ("Coming soon" plus one focusable element) until their tasks land; Settings (§1.2) and Power (§1.4) are real.

### 1.5 Full-screen apps (target; owner 2026-10-28; T-130, T-138, T-147)
- Every app and game runs full screen in the shell's app layer (ADR-002 A, T-130). As built (T-150): `Shell.addApp(item)`, `focusApp`, `removeApp` manage the layer (`appLayer`, z 5: above home and pages, below toasts, modals, the Guide overlay and the volume OSD); the last app item removed or destroyed gives focus back to the page or Home. While an app is in front the shell draws nothing over it except the quick menu, the volume OSD and critical toasts (errors, low controller battery); other toasts wait until the user is back in the shell.
- Launch (Play on Home, a card, or the Store's Open): input to the shell locks, the launch transition and splash run (04 §5.1) until the app maps its first window, then the app gets focus. Failure: error toast, focus back where Play was. After 20 s without a window the splash offers Close.
- Guide (short press) opens the quick menu over the live app; Back or Resume closes it and returns focus to the app; holding Guide still opens the power choices. Home leaves the app running (it becomes the Running card in Recent; Accept on it resumes). Close app asks `Session.stop` and confirms only when the app reports unsaved state (later). One app is in front; others keep running until closed (memory limits: later).

### 1.2 Settings page (as built, T-103)
- Two panes. Left: the category list (My profile, Manage profiles for an admin only, Network, Bluetooth & controllers, Display & motion, Audio, Storage, Accessibility, System, About). Right: the selected category's pane. Up/Down choose a category and swap the pane at once; Right or Accept enters the pane (only if it has content); Left or Back returns to the list; Back on the list closes the page. A pane is built the first time its category is selected (T-349: opening Settings builds only the pane it opens on, My profile or the `params.category` one) and then stays alive, hidden while another category is selected, so each remembers its last focused row. Its modals (PIN pad, keyboard, confirmations, the Resolution / Refresh rate / Wallpaper / Time zone pickers, the pairing screen) are built the first time they open and then kept; a pane's service refreshes wait until it is first shown (the sound themes one frame later). The Network singleton is created when the shell starts, so its list is there when the pane is first shown.
- Inside a pane, Up/Down move between rows (clamped, no wrap); a pane with more rows than fit scrolls to keep the focused row visible, and if the focused row disappears focus moves to a nearby row. A row keeps the keys it uses: a ChoiceRow or SliderRow consumes Left/Right, so those never leave the pane (leave with Back); ToggleRow and SettingRow leave Left to the pane, which returns to the list.
- **Activity (T-247):** Settings -> My profile has an **Activity** row (every signed-in profile) that opens the nested `ActivityPage`. Decision: a nested page like System information, not a Home page, so it stays next to the profile's other personal settings; the app page has a "Your activity" button to the same data per game. Private: it reads the active profile's ledger only (`Usage`, `shell/src/usage/`, over `Session1.GetTotals`/`ListSessions`), forgets everything when the profile changes, and no profile, admin or not, sees another's. The page, top to bottom: **This week** (a `ChartRow`: the total in `typeTitle`, one plain sentence against the week before ("3 h 40 min more than the week before.", "About the same as the week before." within 5 min, never a score), and a seven-bar chart of the last seven days with today highlighted; Left/Right read a bar's value above it ("Fri 50 min") and never leave the row; the screen reader gets the whole week as text, "Sun 20 min, Mon 0 min, ..."), **Most played, last 30 days** (up to five rows: icon, game, "3 h 40 min, last played today"; Accept opens the game's page), **Highlights** (modest facts that appear only when true: the longest session this month; "You have played 3 days in a row." from two days; "You have not played X for 60 days." up to three, which feeds the app-management hints of T-221), **History** (newest first, grouped by day: a day heading "Yesterday, 50 min" and rows "19:40, 40 min"; 60 sessions at most) and **Clear my activity** (a `PowerConfirm` "custom" with Cancel focused, then `Usage.clearHistory()` -> `Session1.ClearHistory`, the same call T-220's "Clear my play history" uses: one implementation; only the active profile). A new profile sees "Nothing here yet. When you play something, the time you spend shows up on this page. Only you can see it." Durations are plain words via `DurationText` ("1 h 20 min", Turkish "1 sa 20 dk"; minutes rounded to the nearest). The **game page** (`ActivityGamePage`, `Shell.openPage("activityGame", { appId })`): time played, times played, first and last played, longest and average session, and a 30-day strip. The week is the last seven days ending today (a rolling week, not Monday to Sunday). Hint bar: `B` Back on the chart and the read-only rows, `A` Select elsewhere. Nothing here ranks, scores or nudges. The shell tells the service who is in front (`UsagePresence.qml`: the newest running app of the active profile while an app item has the screen and the Guide overlay is closed; "idle" after 10 minutes without input; "locked" with nobody signed in; on every change and every 20 s).
- **My profile and Manage profiles** (T-105, split in T-315): two categories instead of one "Profiles" pane. **My profile** (every signed-in profile, key `myprofile`, `MyProfilePane`) is the first category: who I am (name, role, "PIN" when set; read-only), **Rename <name>** (admin and standard, not guest), **Set a PIN / Change my PIN**, **Remove my PIN** (standard with a PIN) and **Activity**; profile customisation (T-210) and the parental limits view (T-212) get their rows here, between Rename and the PIN rows and before Activity. **Manage profiles** (admins only, key `profiles`, `ManageProfilesPane`) is the second category: the list of all profiles (Accept selects one), **Rename / Delete** the selected profile (another one: your own is under My profile, and selecting it only says so), **Add profile** with its form; roles (T-211) and parental controls (T-212) are set with further rows beside Rename / Delete. For anyone else the category is not in the list at all (not greyed out), so Up/Down and the nav keys never reach it; if the signed-in profile stops being an admin while Settings is open, the list loses it, the selected category stays selected when it is still listed, and a focus inside the vanished pane goes to the category list. Deep link: `params.category` `"myprofile"` or `"profiles"` (the latter only lands for an admin). See 03 §1.1 "UI flows". `ProfilePaneBase` holds what both share (PIN pad, name keyboard, delete confirmation, rename/delete flow, message, focus return). Its modals (PIN pad, on-screen keyboard) live in the shell's `modalLayer` (above pages and toasts, below the picker and the Guide overlay); a page reaches it through `Page.overlay`.
- **Display & motion** writes live to `DeviceSettings`: Effects quality (High/Balanced/Performance, cycling), Reduced motion (toggle), Screen edge (overscan, 0-10 % in 1 % steps), **Theme** (T-209: Dreamglass, Midnight, Aurora, Ember, Daylight, cycling with Left/Right; three small chips before the name show the ground, the glass over it and the tint of the theme shown; applies at once with a cross-fade, focus stays on the row; `04` §3.1), and (T-208) **Wallpaper** and **Dim wallpaper**; the Dim row's bar and number show the dim that is applied (T-286): the larger of the slider and the legibility rule for the picture under the theme (`04` 3.5), while Left/Right still step the slider's own position, so a bright picture shows e.g. 54 % with the slider at 20 %, and the row then carries the caption "Raised for this picture" (T-312). The rows follow the setting, including changes made elsewhere.
  **The connected screen (T-198, `Display`, `org.ruyaos.Devices1.Display`)** has four rows after Wallpaper and Dim wallpaper (they come last so that the pane's focus order did not change): **Resolution** (the value is the mode in use, "3840 × 2160"; Accept opens `ModePicker`, a modal list, one row per size, the screen's own choice marked "Recommended", the one in use "Current", opening on the one in use; Up/Down clamp, Accept chooses, Back closes without choosing and focus returns to the row), **Refresh rate** (only while the size in use has more than one rate; the same picker; the rate is kept when a new size has it), **UI size** (a `ChoiceRow`: Auto, 100, 125, 150, 200 %; applies at once, `04` §2) and **Reset display settings** (Accept puts the preferred mode and Auto back; toast "Display settings reset."; the mode part restarts the shell like a change, but needs no confirmation). Resolution and Refresh rate are there only while the service reports a connected output; UI size and Reset are **always** there, so a stored size that is too big can be undone on a screen that reports nothing. A mode change (and a reset that changes the mode) is **refused while a game or app runs** (the restart would kill it: toast "Close running games and apps first...") and while the service says the compositor has restarted too often in the last two minutes (toast "Try again in a minute..."; `03` §2.4). Choosing a mode does not apply it at once: the service writes the KMS config and a marker, the shell **restarts itself** (the compositor unit has `Restart=always`) and comes back on the new mode; it then asks **"Keep this resolution?"** (`ModeConfirm.qml`, hosted by `Shell` at z 40 above every other modal, so it is there whatever the shell shows): the mode in use ("1920 × 1080, 60 Hz"), "If you do nothing, the previous setting comes back in N seconds" counting down from 15 (`Display.confirmSeconds`, one step per `Display.countdownTickMs`), and two buttons, **Revert** and **Keep**. **Focus is on Revert** (lead decision 2026-11-07, black-screen safety: a TV that cannot show the mode shows nothing, and a blind A must go back; Keep needs Right, then A). Accept is **ignored for the first second** after the dialog appears (`Display.acceptGraceMs`), so the second press of a double press on the picker's row cannot keep a mode nobody has seen; B and the countdown work at once. B or Back, Revert, or the countdown reaching zero restore the previous mode and restart the shell again, **whatever the service answers** (a failed Revert restarts too: the compositor's guard at the next start drops a marker that is still there, or rewrites the config from the stored choice); nobody has to see the screen for the revert to happen. A Keep the service refuses shows an error toast and the countdown goes on, so it still reverts. The dialog is modal (every key is taken; Left/Right move between the buttons), its card and texts are capped to the screen width so they wrap at 200 %, and the hint bar's A names the focused button (A Revert / A Keep, B Revert). The shell opens Settings -> Display & motion under it when it comes back, so a Keep returns focus there. If the shell never got to ask (it crashed, or the console was powered off on a black screen), the compositor's next start drops the choice (`03` §2.4) and the shell says once "The screen did not answer, so the previous resolution is back." (warning toast; asked again when the service appears, not only at start). A refused change ("The screen mode could not be changed.", error toast) shows no dialog. Only the first connected output is used.
  **Wallpaper** (T-208; decided to live here rather than in a new "Appearance" category: the list already scrolls at nine, and the wallpaper is a display look like the effects tier): the row shows the current name ("Default (animated blobs)", "Dusk", a copy's file name) and Accept opens the picker, a modal in the `modalLayer` (hint bar: B Cancel). The grid has four columns of 320 px thumbnails: the default look, the six bundled pictures, then the owner's copies (at most 12), the current one badged "Current", focus on it when the picker opens (the focus ring is drawn outside the art at `focusRingOffset`, like the primary buttons, so it reads on a white thumbnail and in the dark Daylight ring). Arrows move (clamped, no wrap), Accept chooses it and closes, Back closes without choosing; Down from the last row goes to the buttons under the grid: **Add from USB drive...** (only while a mounted removable drive has pictures: the shell scans every mounted removable volume, again whenever the volume list changes) and **Delete** (only while a copy is highlighted; the wallpaper falls back to the default look when the deleted copy was the current one; a toast "Wallpaper deleted"). Add shows the drive's pictures in the same grid (title "Pictures on the USB drive", listed by file name with a thumbnail, sorted by name: jpg, png and webp files (webp only where Qt's image-formats plugin is installed: the image does not carry it yet, so there a webp file is simply not listed) directly inside a folder named `Wallpapers` or `Pictures` of the volume, any case, no deeper, regular files only, at most 25 MB and 40 megapixels each, at most 60 listed; nothing on the drive is ever run). Accept on one (only a picture of the current list is accepted; names are drawn as plain text, never markup; at most 200 files are looked at per folder) copies it into the shell's own state directory (`Wallpapers.directory`: `~/.local/share/ruya-shell/wallpapers`, or `RUYA_WALLPAPER_DIR`) with the grid showing "Copying..." and every key swallowed until it ends: the picture is scaled to cover the screen (never enlarged, at most 3840x2160), flattened and re-encoded as JPEG quality 88 on a worker thread, named by the hash of the result (the same picture twice is one copy), and listed in `index.json` with its name and brightness. On success the copy becomes the wallpaper, a toast "Wallpaper added", and the picker returns to the library on it; on failure the grid stays with the reason ("You can keep up to 12 wallpapers. Delete one first." at the limit, "This picture is too large.", "This kind of picture is not supported.", "This picture could not be copied.") and a toast "Could not add the wallpaper". Back in the drive's pictures returns to the library. Removing the drive loses nothing (the copy is ours) and hides Add. **Dim wallpaper** (shown while a wallpaper is set): a slider 0-60 % in 5 % steps, default 20 %; a bright picture (or, under Daylight, a dark one) is veiled further than the slider says (`04` §3.5). If the drive is pulled while Add from USB drive has focus, focus moves to the grid. Per-profile wallpapers are not built: profile homes are 0700 and the shell cannot read them.
- **System** (T-109, T-135): the Language row, then an "Updates" group over `Update1` (mock only for now): a read-only Version row ("0.0.18 · Stable"), an admin-only "Update channel" row (Stable/Beta), an admin-only "Restart automatically for updates" switch (on by default: the service restarts into a staged update at night when the console is idle, `01` §4), and one update row that follows the state: "Check for updates" (value: last check / "Up to date" / "Failed"), "Download version X" once a check found one, "Downloading version X" with the percentage, then "Restart to finish" (Accept opens the restart confirm; the row keeps focus through all of it). Admins also get "Roll back to the previous version" when `rollback_available`: admin PIN (`Profiles.verifyAdmin`), then a confirm, then `Update.rollback()`. When an update becomes `staged` a toast "Update ready. Restart to finish." appears once. **Time zone (T-213):** an admin-only row under Language (value: the zone, "Europe/Istanbul"); Accept opens `ZonePicker.qml`, a modal (`pane.overlay`, hint `B` Cancel) with the same region and city lists as the wizard, opening on the current zone (marked "Current"). Hint bar `A` Select, `B` Cancel (any modal in the modal layer may set `modalHints`; `WallpaperPicker` does too; others keep `B` Cancel). Accept on a city asks `Devices1.Time.SetTimezone`, closes the picker, focus returns to the row, and the result is a toast: "Time zone set to Europe/Istanbul" (success), "Only an admin can do this." (`forbidden`) or "Could not change the time zone." Back on a region closes without choosing. Below the row a dim note "The clock has not been set from the internet yet, so the time may be wrong." shows while `TimeZone.ntpSynchronised` is false, to everyone. **The clock in the status cluster** is `TimeZone.clockText` ("HH:mm", computed from the zone name with `QTimeZone`, not from the process's local time, so a zone change shows at once; refreshed every ten seconds); 12/24 hour stays as it was (24 hour).
  **Device health** (T-200, `Thermal`, `org.ruyaos.Devices1.Thermal`) is the last group of System: one read-only row per sensor, named by class and numbered when there are several ("Processor 33 °C", "Drive 41 °C", "Fan 1820 rpm"), in the service's order (processor, graphics, drives, fans), updated in place (focus stays on a row while its value changes; if the focused row goes away focus moves to a neighbour). A sensor whose level is not normal gets a small round mark and the word ("Warm" in `warningText`, "Hot" or "Critical" in `errorText`; colour is never the only signal). While the worst level is not normal a line under the heading says what the console does (warm: nothing needs to be done; hot: effects are reduced; critical: apps are closed and it may power off), and the **System category** in the list carries the same mark. No sensors (a VM): "No temperature sensors were found on this device." What the shell does at each level is in `01` §2.4; **Display & motion** keeps showing the owner's quality while the console is limited for heat.
- **About**: image version (the `version` the shell was started with), Qt version (runtime, from the `SystemInfo` singleton), services mode (mock or D-Bus), the licence line, and a last row **System information** that opens the full page (T-226). The short summary stays here; the page is a Settings sub-page (`Shell.openPage("systemInfo")`, pushed over Settings, B returns to the About row), not a category of its own: About is where people look for "what is this machine", and System stays for things that can be changed.
- **System information (T-226, `SystemInfoPage.qml`, `SystemInfoRows.qml`).** Read-only, readable from the sofa: label on the left, value on the right, nine groups with a heading each: **Console** (system and version, update channel, image and digest where readable, deployment, kernel, uptime, install month, firmware, Secure Boot, maker and model), **Processor** (model, cores, threads, clock now and highest, temperature), **Memory** (total, in use with a usage bar, available, swap, zram), **Graphics** (GPU, driver and version, Mesa or NVIDIA version, the session's graphics API and renderer string, the visual-effects tier in use, display connector, resolution and refresh rate or the display's preferred mode, temperature), **Storage** (per internal drive: model, type, size, health for NVMe only; the console's free space), **Network** (connection, Wi-Fi name, IP, MAC, link speed or signal), **Bluetooth** (adapter, USB id, address, power), **Audio** (outputs, the default marked), **Controllers** (connected, with battery). A value that cannot be read has no row (never "0"); a group without rows is not shown. HDR and VRR rows are not there yet (T-218/T-222). Numbers other services own are read from them and not repeated by the System service: temperatures (`Thermal`, T-200), the console's free space (`Storage.internal`, T-196), controller batteries (`Batteries`, T-223; a connected Bluetooth controller without an entry there is listed with its own battery), the connection (`Network`), outputs (`Audio`), the channel (`Update`). Every row takes focus only so that Up/Down scroll (whole rows, look-ahead like every settings pane); Accept on a row does nothing; B or Left goes back. While the page is visible it asks for the live values (clock, memory use, uptime) every `SystemDetails.liveIntervalMs` (2 s) and updates the rows in place, so focus stays; the timer runs only while the page is visible. The list model holds row ids only (rows are bindings), so a language switch or a live value never rebuilds a row; a reply older than a newer one is dropped (`RequestSequence`). **Report:** the first rows are **Save report to USB drive** (writes `RuyaOS-system-report.txt` and `RuyaOS-system-report.json` to the first mounted, writable removable drive through `Devices1.Storage.SaveReport`; a message says where, or why not: no drive, read-only, full, cannot be written) and **Include network addresses** (off every time the page opens). Without it the MAC address, IP address, Wi-Fi name and Bluetooth address are not in either file (their rows are left out when the report is composed, so they never reach the service), and the file says so. The files are English whatever the UI language and the JSON carries the raw values (sizes in bytes). A note says that the report is only a file and nothing is sent anywhere. With several drives plugged in the first is used (a chooser is an Inbox line).
- Reusable controls live in `shell/qml/controls/` (`SettingControl` base, `SettingRow`, `ToggleRow`, `ChoiceRow`, `SliderRow`); see `shell/CLAUDE.md`.

### 1.4a Power saving (T-202; the timeline and the inhibitors are in `01` §2.2)
- **One idle clock** (`IdleClock.qml`, a singleton): `KeyRouter.activity` (any key, controller button, the Guide button, pointer or touch event the window receives) calls `IdleClock.noteInput()`; the clock reads **a monotonic source** (`MonotonicClock`, a `QElapsedTimer`; never the wall clock, so NTP, a time zone or a date set by hand changes no stage, T-293); `idleSeconds` is the time since, `quietSeconds` the same with held-awake time counted as activity, and `stage` is `active`, `saver`, `off` or `sleep`. The update report (`Update.noteActivity`), `LiquidGate`, the toasts and `UsagePresence` hear input from its `inputNoted` signal; none keeps a clock of its own. The 60-second throttle of `Update.noteActivity` only limits bus calls.
- **Screen saver** (`ScreenSaver.qml`, z 100 above everything, the OSD and dialogs included): a near-black ground (`Theme.saverGround`, the same in every theme, so it is dark on Daylight too), the installed apps' art (hero, else tile, else the generated art) at `Theme.saverArtOpacity` drifting and cross-fading every `Theme.saverSlideMs`, and a clock (`hh:mm`) that takes a new place every minute inside the safe area (burn-in safe). No focus ring and nothing takes focus. **Input**: `KeyRouter.swallowInput` is on while any stage but `active` is up: the first key, button, pointer or touch event is reported to the clock and then eaten (with its release, and the repeats of a held key), so it wakes the console and reaches no page or app; the page keeps the focus it had. A key that was eaten and not seen released is forgotten when the window is deactivated or after 2 s without a repeat, so a lost release cannot eat a later press. **Performance tier**: no art, only the dark ground and the moving clock. **Reduced motion**: one still dimmed frame (no drift, no cross-fade); the clock still jumps to its new place. **PIN rule (decision)**: the saver starts even over a PIN pad, but every open `PinPad` is cancelled when it starts (`IdleClock.saverStarted`), so nothing typed is left and no field is active behind it; the picker is back on its list after waking.
- **Display off**: stage `off`: a black frame, the clock hidden, no timer or animation running, the backdrop paused (`Backdrop.shown`; also paused, `Backdrop.paused`, while an app is in front and covers Home, T-293); `ScreenPower.setOn(false)` switches the display off through the platform plugin (`QPlatformScreen::setPowerState`, DPMS under `eglfs_kms`; on other platforms only the black frame). The first input switches it on and wakes, and **input in the second after the screen came back is eaten too** (`IdleClock.wakeGapMs` = 1000, a constant in `IdleClock`, to be tuned on the Dell; `IdleClock.swallowInput` = `covered || wakeGuard` feeds `KeyRouter.swallowInput`): a press while the TV is still re-syncing only counts as activity. It applies after the dark screen and after sleep, not after the saver. How a TV re-syncs is for the Dell to record (T-202 Outcome).
- **Sleep**: stage `sleep`: `Power.suspend()`. Not entered unless `Power.sleepAllowed`. **A resume restarts the clock from `active`** in every stage: the monotonic clock stops while the machine sleeps, so the resume is read from `MonotonicClock.sleptMs` (CLOCK_BOOTTIME minus CLOCK_MONOTONIC on Linux; 0 elsewhere), which jumps across a suspend; a clock that does count the sleep shows as a gap above `gapMs` and is read the same way.
- **Idle controllers**: every minute while the console has been idle for the controller time, and at once when input ends an idle period, the shell sends `Bluetooth.reportIdle(idle, timeout, hold)` (`Devices1.Bluetooth.ReportIdle`). `hold` is true while an app is in front (the shell does not see every button a game uses: X, Y, the triggers and the right stick are not mapped by `ruya-inputd`) or something holds the console awake (an install or update included, so a pad is never switched off during an install or update screen). The service (`idle_pads.rs`) disconnects each connected, paired controller once per idle period; a pad that reconnects gets the whole time again. The idle time is the console's, not the pad's: with two pads, input on one keeps both on.
- **Settings -> Power saving** (an own category before System; `PowerSavingPane`): Screen saver (Off, 5, 10, 15, 30 minutes), Turn the screen off (Never, 15, 30 minutes, 1 hour), Sleep (Never, 30 minutes, 1, 2, 4 hours; where logind allows suspend but `/usr/share/ruyaos/suspend-verified` does not exist yet the row stays, reads "Sleep is not available on this console yet", is disabled (takes no focus, no value) and the step does not exist; without `CanSuspend` the row is hidden), Controllers turn off (Never, 5, 10, 15, 30 minutes) and a read-only "Keeping the console awake" row that names what holds the console awake ("a download or install", "a system update", "audio playing", "an app"; the log has the exact reasons). Each row writes `DeviceSettings` at once and the clock follows without a restart. When a later step is set at or before an earlier one (for example the screen off at 15 minutes with the saver at 30) one extra row says so ("The screen saver never shows: The screen turns off first"; also for sleep: "The screen is not turned off: The console sleeps instead"); the choices stay free. A Power mode row (Balanced, Power saver, Performance) waits for `power-profiles-daemon` in the image. The saver's clock is `TimeZone.formatClock` (the console's zone, like the status bar), not the process zone. The look is in `04` §5 (Screen saver) and its tokens in §3.1 and §3.4.

## 2. Visual language
Defined in [`04-design-system.md`](04-design-system.md) ("Dreamglass", ADR-012): principles, colour/type/spacing/radius/motion tokens, focus, depth, the component catalogue, copy rules and the screenshot review list. In short: dark theme, 10-foot type (body 30 px, min 24 px at 1080p), 5 % safe area, frosted glass over blurred hero art, the focused item scales to 1.08 with a white ring that is never hidden, and every value is a `Theme` token.

## 3. Motion & effects

### 3.1 Techniques
| Technique | Use | Caveats |
|---|---|---|
| `Behavior on x/opacity/scale` | Implicit motion for focus, cards | Don't combine with an explicit animation on the same property |
| `states` + `transitions` | Page and panel layouts | Keep the state count small and named |
| `Sequential/ParallelAnimation` | Choreographed sequences (launch, boot-in) | Must be interruptible: a new input cancels and settles |
| Animate `opacity`, `scale`, `x/y`, `rotation` only | GPU-cheap | Avoid animating `width/height`, layout or text |
| `layer.enabled` / `ShaderEffectSource` | Cache static subtrees and the **single shared blurred backdrop** | Costs VRAM; invalidate rarely |
| `MultiEffect` (blur, shadow, colourise) | Frosted panels, glow | Keep `blurMax` low on low tier; blur a downscaled source |
| Custom `ShaderEffect` | Special effects | Shaders precompiled to `.qsb` via `qt_add_shaders()` in CMake; **no inline GLSL in Qt 6** |

Blur pattern: render the backdrop (hero art) once to a quarter-resolution `ShaderEffectSource`, apply `MultiEffect` blur to that, and share the result among all panels. Re-blur only when the hero art changes, with a crossfade.

### 3.2 Durations
Focus move 120–160 ms, page transition 250–350 ms, overlay open 200 ms, all ease-out. These are defined as tokens. **Reduced motion** sets the `focusMs`, `pageMs` and `tintMs` tokens to 0, removes the page slide (`pageSlide` = 0) and stops the backdrop drift. (Implemented as 0, not 60 ms crossfades.) Every duration token is its base value times `motionScale`, which is 0 under reduced motion (T-204). The card glare and the art-placeholder shimmer (04 §3.8) do not run either, while shadows stay. The feedback animations are also off under reduced motion: the PIN-pad wrong-PIN shake and the Accept pulse on art cards do not run at all (the error message and sound, or the launch toast, remain). Their sizes and durations are `Theme` tokens (`shakeDistance`, `shakeQuickMs`, `shakeSlowMs`, `pulseScale`, `pulseDownMs`, `pulseUpMs`). The setting is per device (`DeviceSettings`), and `--reduced-motion` forces it for a session.

### 3.3 Quality tiers
`Effects = High | Balanced | Performance`, chosen automatically at first boot from the GPU (renderer string plus a quick benchmark) and overridable in Settings. **Implemented so far:** the per-device `effectsQuality` setting and the `--effects` flag; automatic detection is still open (needs real hardware).

**Heat (T-200):** when the console runs hot the shell drops to Performance for the session (`DeviceSettings.thermalLimited`: `effectsQuality` reads `performance`, `storedEffectsQuality` keeps the owner's choice) until the temperatures are normal again (`01` §2.4).

Token values per tier (`Theme.qml`): `blurMax` is 64 / 32 / 0, and the glass `MultiEffect`s (`GlassPill`) are hidden at 0, where the pills use the more opaque `glassOpaque` token. Ambient drift of the backdrop runs on High and Balanced, and stops on Performance. On High the glass pills also draw the liquid look (`Theme.liquidPill`, 04 §3.8). Overscan (`safeArea`) is a per-device fraction 0.0 to 0.10, default 0.05.

| | High | Balanced | Performance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Backdrop blur | Full, ½-res source | ¼-res source | None (dim + static pre-blurred image) |
| Parallax / ambient motion | On | Reduced | Off |
| Art resolution | 4K variants | 1080p | 720p |

## 4. Input & focus model

### 4.1 Event path
Physical pad → input daemon (ADR-003) → shell. While the shell has input focus, the daemon emits navigation as key events (Up/Down/Left/Right, Accept, Back, Menu, Guide, LB/RB, LT/RT) to the compositor's seat. While an app is focused, only **Guide** (and the volume keys) reach the shell. As built (T-150): the C++ `KeyRouter` (event filter on the window, `shell/src/input/`) takes the Guide keys, including Super and the Guide hold, from a focused app item and hands them to the shell; the volume keys are application shortcuts matched before any key event; every other key reaches the app by normal focus delivery (the router counts focus on the app item or any item inside it). In the image (T-130) the app items are `ShellSurfaceItem`s, one per xdg toplevel, which forward the keys they get to their Wayland client. `KeyRouter.isGuideKey` is the one definition of which keys are Guide. Keyboard and mouse go through libinput and the compositor as normal.

### 4.2 Mapping
| Action | Xbox | PlayStation | Keyboard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accept | A | ✕ (configurable to ○ for JP layout) | Enter |
| Back | B | ○ | Esc / Backspace |
| Context menu | Menu (☰) | Options | `M` / context key |
| Guide / overlay | Xbox | PS | `Super` (left or right; holding it opens the power choices) |
| Tab switch | LB/RB | L1/R1 | `Q`/`E`, PgUp/PgDn |
| Fast scroll | LT/RT, stick held | L2/R2 | Home/End |

As built (T-179): the singleton `BackKey` (`shell/qml/BackKey.qml`) is the one definition of which keys are Back. `BackKey.isBackOrBackspace(key)` (Back, Esc, Backspace) is what pages, Settings panes, the section nav, the setup wizard, the Guide overlay and the confirm dialogs use. The text fields (`TextEntry`, `PinPad`) use `BackKey.isBack(key)` (Back, Esc) only, because there Backspace deletes a character and never goes back. Back acts on every key event, as Esc always did; it does not filter auto-repeat.

### 4.3 Rules
- Every screen has exactly one `FocusScope` root, and every interactive item is reachable with the D-pad. Directional navigation uses `KeyNavigation` or a small spatial-navigation helper (nearest item in the direction pressed).
- Focus is never lost. When the focused item disappears, focus moves to the nearest sibling, else to the scope's default.
- Focus is restored per surface and per profile (returning to Home focuses the last-launched tile).
- Key repeat on hold: 400 ms delay, then accelerating repeat. The analog stick maps to the same, with speed proportional to deflection.
- Long-press (≥ 500 ms) on Accept opens the context menu, with a visible progress ring.
- Modal dialogs trap focus. The PIN pad accepts only digits, Back and Accept. While its profile is locked out the digits, Del and OK are dimmed and ignored, and the only message is the countdown; the pad works again when it reaches 0.
- On the profile picker, any controller may act. After login, the logged-in profile's controller is "player 1".
- **Hint bar (T-158).** One `HintBar` in the shell's top layer says what the buttons do on the screen on top. The screen supplies the hints: a page through `Page.hints` (default `A` Select, `B` Back; a page may override it), Home `A` Select and one `LB RB` Sections hint (two glyphs, one verb), the setup wizard its own hints (`Setup.hints`: `A` Next, or Select on the profile step, `B` Back except on the first and last step; `B` Cancel over its PIN pad (its keyboard supplies its own hint, below); `B` Skip, with `A` Try again after a timeout, on the controller pairing stage), a page with nothing to select (placeholder pages and categories without a pane) only `B` Back (`Page.backOnlyHints`); Settings > About and System information show `B` Back only while a read-only row has focus and `A` Select on the rows that act (the System information row, Save report, Include network addresses), the Guide overlay `A` Select and `B` Close (`B` Back in the power choices), a modal whose focus is in the modal layer or the picker's PIN pad `B` Cancel (the on-screen keyboard says `B` Delete while a controller's Back would delete a letter and `B` Cancel when the field is empty or after typing on a physical keyboard; it supplies that itself as `TextEntry.entryHints`, T-194), the picker `A` Select. It is hidden while an app is focused (a Guide overlay over an app still shows its own), never takes focus, and on Home sits under the Recent row.
- The pointer is shown only after mouse movement and hidden after 3 s idle.

### 4.4 Text entry (as built, T-104)
`TextEntry.qml` is the modal on-screen keyboard; every flow that needs text uses it. `open(title, initialText, { maxLength, mask, layout })`, signals `accepted(text)` and `cancelled()`. `layout` is `text`, `email` (adds `@`, `.`, `.com`) or `url` (adds `/`, `.`, `.com`); `mask` shows dots but `accepted` carries the real text; `maxLength` 0 means unlimited. The layout is data (`layoutFor`): rows of keys with a width in key units, on a letters page and a symbols page (the `#+=`/`ABC` key switches).

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| D-pad / stick | Move over the keys. Left/Right wrap within a row; Up/Down go to the key whose centre is nearest in x (ties go left; no wrap vertically) |
| Accept (A, `Return`) | Press the focused key |
| Back (B, `Escape`) | Delete one character; cancel when the field is empty (`Backspace` never cancels, see below) |
| Start (`Menu`) / keypad `Enter` | Done (does nothing while the field is empty) |
| LB / RB (`PageUp` / `PageDown`) | Jump to Del / Space |
| Shift key | One-shot capital for the next character |
| Physical keyboard | Printable characters insert; `Backspace` deletes |

A controller's A and B arrive as `Return` and `Escape`, the same keys a keyboard sends. So once the user types on a physical keyboard (a printable key or `Backspace`), `Return` means Done and `Escape` means Cancel, until the next D-pad key switches back to controller behaviour. The field is append/delete at the end only. The component swallows every key (including Guide and volume keys' key events) and returns focus to the item that had it when it opened. Not yet used by any flow (T-105 is the first).

## 5. Audio (UI side)
- Channels: **UI SFX** (the cues of §5.3), **Ambient** (the loop of §5.3; on by default at the slider's default level, owner to confirm after listening), **Media/App** (everything else).
- These are implemented as PipeWire stream roles (`media.role = Notification|Music|Game|Movie`). WirePlumber policy does **ducking**: Ambient is lowered by about 8 dB while a UI cue or app audio plays, and Ambient is muted whenever an app is in the foreground.
- SFX playback uses `QSoundEffect` with preloaded WAVs. Configure a low PipeWire quantum for the SFX stream to hit the latency KPI.
- Separate volume sliders for UI, Ambient and Master. The volume OSD appears on any volume change, including controller or keyboard media keys.
- Mild stereo panning of navigation SFX by focus x-position (on in High, off otherwise).
- Output switching (HDMI / analog / USB / Bluetooth) follows WirePlumber defaults with a "prefer most recently connected" rule. The shell shows a toast. Covered in 03 §2.3.

### 5.1 Toasts and volume OSD (as built, T-108)
- `Notifications.show(text, kind, iconText)` (QML singleton; kind `info|success|warning|error`, unknown kinds become `info`) queues a toast. `ToastLayer` shows at most 3 at the top right, below the header and inside the safe area; the rest wait in order. Each toast dismisses itself after `Theme.toastMs` (4000 ms), fading and sliding with `Theme.pageMs`/`pageSlide` (so reduced motion removes the movement).
- **Sticky toasts (T-159):** `Notifications.showSticky(key, text, kind)` shows a toast that does not dismiss itself (no timer) and stays until `Notifications.remove(key)`; one per key (showing the key again replaces it), and it can be removed while it still waits in the queue. The first user is the restart notice: while `Update.restartPending` (the service's `restart_at` is set, `01` §4) a `warning` toast "Restarting to finish the update in 2 minutes. Press any button to wait." is up, and it goes when the record clears it. `Update.noteActivity` reports every input to the service while a restart is pending (instead of once a minute), because that press is the one that calls the restart off.
- `Audio` (C++ singleton over `org.ruyaos.Devices1.Audio`, see §5.2) holds the master `volume` (0-100) and `muted`, with `setVolume`, `stepVolume(delta)`, `toggleMute()` and `setMuted()`. `adjusted()` fires after every master command, even a clamped one (and when the master level or mute changes from outside), but not for a channel volume alone.
- **Volume keys while muted** (T-114): Volume Up unmutes and raises; Volume Down stays muted and changes nothing (the OSD shows "Muted"); Mute toggles only the mute.
- `VolumeOsd` (bottom centre) appears on `Audio.adjusted` and hides `Theme.osdMs` (1500 ms) after the last one. It shows the level and a "Muted" state.
- Volume Up/Down/Mute are `Shortcut`s (application context) at the `Shell` root, so they work in every state, modals included, step by 5, and never move focus. Toasts and the OSD are display-only and never take focus.
- Stacking: toasts (z 10) sit above pages and below the modals (profile picker, Guide overlay: z 20); the OSD is on top (z 30).

## 6. Asset pipeline
- Per app, the art set (from AppStream/`x-ruya-*` keys, or our fallback generator) is icon (SVG or 256 px), tile (16:9), hero (16:9, ≥ 1920 px), logo (transparent), and an optional background.
- On install, `ruya-store` produces resized variants (720p/1080p/2160p) in WebP into `/var/cache/ruyaos/art/`. The shell loads only the variant matching the quality tier and display, via a `QQuickImageProvider` (`image://art/<appid>/<kind>`).
- `Image { asynchronous: true; cache: true; sourceSize: … }` always. Show a placeholder or blurhash while loading.
- **Budget:** ≤ 100 MB of textures on the low tier and ≤ 300 MB on mid/high. Off-screen rows unload their art. One shared blurred backdrop.
- Shell-owned assets (sounds, icons, fonts) are compiled into the binary as Qt resources.

## 7. Accessibility & internationalisation
- Text scale (100–200%), high-contrast theme, reduced motion, mono audio, focus-ring size.
- Screen reader: expose `Accessible.*` properties everywhere. Orca support on a custom compositor is **unknown**, so spike it in Phase 2 and record the result.
- Subtitles are app-owned. The shell only offers a system-wide preference that it passes to apps where a standard exists.
- All strings use `qsTr()`. Translations are Qt `.ts` files managed in a translation platform. Layouts use anchors/`LayoutMirroring` so RTL works (tested with a pseudo-RTL locale). Initial locales: en, tr (others later).
- **Built (T-109).** Translations are `shell/i18n/ruyashell_<lang>.ts` (`qt_add_translations`, embedded as `:/i18n/ruyashell_<lang>.qm`; English is the source language and its file carries the plural forms). `DeviceSettings.language` (`en`/`tr`) installs the `QTranslator` and calls `QQmlEngine::retranslate()`, so changing it in Settings -> System (or the first-boot language step) re-translates the open UI at once. Default: the stored value, else the system locale, else `en`. Messages stored in a property when an event happened (PIN-pad and Settings status lines, toasts) keep their language until the next event. Lists that are models never change with the language (they hold keys; the labels are bindings), because a changed model would reset `currentIndex` and lose focus. The on-screen keyboard follows the language: Turkish adds ç ğ ı ö ş ü and the Q layout's extra keys (narrower keys, rows stay 10 units); Shift maps i to İ and ı to I. `ruya-shell --reverse` lays the UI out right-to-left (`LayoutMirroring`), a readiness check: sign-in, the home tiles and Settings stay navigable (tested); the remaining RTL gaps are listed in the T-109 Outcome. A completeness lint (`scripts/lint-translations.ps1`) fails on unfinished entries.

## 9. Performance rules
1. Never block the GUI thread. All I/O goes over async D-Bus calls. `WorkerScript` is for pure CPU work only.
2. Bindings and JavaScript should take ≤ 2 ms per frame. Profile with the QML Profiler, GammaRay and `perf` after each feature.
3. Hide off-screen items (`visible: false`). Don't put alpha on fullscreen layers except the one backdrop.
4. Keep V-Sync on. Never set a custom render loop frame rate.
5. Test every effect change on the low tier before merging (CI KPI job on the reference box).

### 5.3 Sound themes, the cue set and the ambient bed (T-227; the maker's guide is `docs/developers/sound-themes.md`)
- **A sound theme** is a folder with `theme.json` (`name`, `author`, `licence` as an SPDX id, `version`, `cues`: cue name to file, optional `ambient`: `file`, `loop` {`start`, `end`} in ms, `gain` 0-1), the audio files and a README. Cues: WAV PCM 16-bit, mono or stereo, 48 kHz, at most 1.5 s and 200 KB, peak below -3 dBFS. Ambient: Ogg Vorbis, Ogg Opus or FLAC, at most 3 MB, a loop of at least 5 s inside the file. `theme.json` at most 64 KB, at most 64 cues. A path in the manifest is plain and relative (no `..`, no drive letter, no link). **The checker** (`src/sound/SoundThemeCheck.cpp`, a pure function from a folder to ok/problems/warnings) enforces all of this reading the folder as data only: sizes before any read, WAV parsed in memory, the ambient file read at its head and tail for its length (FLAC STREAMINFO, the Ogg identification header and the last page's granule), and for FLAC the header of the first audio frame after the metadata blocks, which must name its sample rate (rate code 0, "as in STREAMINFO", is refused: Qt 6.11's FFmpeg player opens its output at 0 Hz for it, the bed never sounds and Qt retries several times a second, T-321), nothing decoded by a codec library, nothing executed. **The ambient must be heard on a TV and a 5 cm speaker (T-330):** a FLAC ambient (read whole, at most 3 MB) is decoded by the checker's own decoder (RFC 9639: every subframe type, every stereo layout; the CRC-8 and CRC-16 of every frame must match, every count and shift is checked before use, a sample must fit its bit depth; at most 10 minutes of audio are decoded whatever STREAMINFO claims) over the first minute of the loop, and refused when more than half of its energy is below 150 Hz (a 4th-order Butterworth low-pass at 150 Hz) or when the median RMS of its 1 s windows is below -40 dBFS (before the gain), and when a frame is damaged or the audio ends before the loop does. An Ogg ambient is not decoded; it passes with a warning that its level and bass were not checked. The bundled beds (`scripts/make-sounds.py`) have their energy between 200 Hz and 4 kHz at -22.5 LUFS, true peak below -3 dBFS, and the generator's `--verify` fails outside -26 to -22 LUFS, above 3 % below 120 Hz or under 85 % between 200 Hz and 4 kHz. The shell, `ruya-shell --check-sound-theme <folder>` and `scripts/lint-sound-themes.ps1` use this one function. The ambient formats are Ogg and FLAC because the bundled theme is generated by a script that has a FLAC encoder but no Vorbis one.
- **Cue set** (the names are the contract): `navigate` (focus moves), `select`, `back`, `error` (wrong PIN, a refused action), `notification` (a toast), `warning` (a warning or error toast, a sticky toast), `open`/`close` (the Guide overlay), `page` (a section changes), `volume` (the volume bar), `startup` (once, at the first frame the shell draws), `toggle-on`/`toggle-off` (a switch). `Sfx.qml` has one function per cue (`toggled(checked)` picks the toggle cue) and a counter for each; `playedCount` counts the cues that really sounded. A cue the active theme does not name plays from the bundled theme; a cue no theme has is silent. The UI sounds channel scales every cue (navigate keeps its 0.6 base, volume 0.8); reduced motion does not silence sound, UI sounds at 0 does (the request is counted, nothing plays and the ambient is not ducked). **No cue plays while the screen saver, the dark screen or sleep is up** (`IdleClock.covered`). Where a toast, a wrong PIN or a refusal has its own cue, the older `back` sound was taken out so one event makes one sound.
- **Where themes live**: the bundled theme "Dreamglass" is under `/usr/share/ruyaos/sounds/dreamglass/` (installed by `shell/CMakeLists.txt`). The Windows dev build and the Linux test build also embed the theme as a Qt resource (CMake option `RUYA_SOUNDS_RESOURCE`, ON there); the image build (`RUYA_COMPOSITOR=ON`) leaves it out, so `ruya-shell` does not carry a second copy of 2.5 MB (1.1 MB before the T-330 bed), and a missing image folder then means the bundled theme is unusable (a log line, no cues). A USB drive's themes are `RuyaOS/sounds/<folder>/` (folder names of letters, digits, `.`, `_`, `-`; at most 16 looked at per drive; the checker runs in a worker thread, `SoundThemes.refresh`). Choosing one **copies** it (all regular files, no links, at most 100 files and 12 MB, exactly that much is fine; a theme over a limit is refused as "too-many-files" or "too-large" with its own toast; re-checked after copying) to the shell's data directory (`<AppData>/sounds/<folder>`, `RUYA_SOUND_DIR` overrides it); at most 8 copies; nothing is ever loaded from the drive in place. A chosen theme that fails the checker is ignored with a log line and a warning toast, and the bundled theme plays (`SoundThemes.selectedBroken`). The choice is `DeviceSettings.soundTheme` (`dreamglass` or `user:<folder>`, per device).
- **Ambient bed** (`AmbientLoop.qml`, in `Shell`): plays on Home and the menus (also the profile picker) at the Ambient channel's level times the theme's `gain`; the switch `DeviceSettings.ambientSound` (default on) turns it off. It fades out over 800 ms when an app is in front or the screen saver, the dark screen or sleep starts, and the players are paused when the fade has finished; it fades in again on return. The fade is sound, not motion, so reduced motion does not shorten it. It is lowered by -8 dB (`SoundThemes.duckGain`, ramp `duckRampMs` 120 ms) while a cue sounds: the hold is the cue's own length from the checker (`SoundThemes.cueMs`), at least `duckMs` 450 ms. All audio timing (fade, duck hold and ramp, cross-fade, loop poll) is a constant of `SoundThemes` (`04` 3.9). The loop is two `MediaPlayer`s that take turns: the second starts at the loop start `crossMs` (1.5 s) before the first reaches the loop end and the two cross-fade, so the join never clicks whatever the codec does at a file's end. A theme without `ambient` has no ambient sound (there is no fallback for it). The shell fades and ducks the bed itself; WirePlumber's role ducking/foreground-mute (02 5 first paragraph) is not configured on the image yet and, once it is, may not stack beyond -8 dB in total: decide by listening on the Dell.
- **Settings -> Audio** after the sliders: the **Ambient sound** switch under the Ambient slider, and at the end the **Sound theme** choice (the bundled theme and the copies; Left/Right) followed by one row for every valid theme on a mounted USB drive ("Use "Name" from the USB drive", Accept copies it and switches to it, toast "Sound theme: Name"). The drive rows follow the drives in place, so focus stays; a theme that fails the checker has no theme row but a row "Can't use "Name"" whose value says why in one plain line (`SoundThemeText.qml` turns the checker's first problem into words, en and tr: "A cue is louder than allowed", "The theme.json file has a mistake", ...; never a file name or path); Accept repeats it as a toast, and a copy the checker refuses after copying says the same in its toast. When the chosen theme is a copy, the row **Delete "Name"** sits right under the choice row (never for the bundled theme): Accept opens the confirmation (`PowerConfirm`, Cancel has focus), Confirm resets the setting to Dreamglass and deletes the folder (`SoundThemes.removeTheme`), with no cue of its own, no toast, and focus on the Sound theme row; Back leaves the confirmation and keeps the theme. The toast for "8 copies already" reads "Delete one first, or copy over one with the same name".
- **Dreamglass** is generated by `scripts/make-sounds.py` from numbers (sine partials with a rounded attack for the bells, detuned sine layers with slow level drift for the pad, a 75 s loop in which every frequency and every drift is a whole number of cycles, so the last sample joins the first), CC0, no outside samples. `--verify` (in `dev.ps1 test`) checks determinism, the limits, the seam and that the files in the repository are what the script writes.
